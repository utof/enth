# The P1a module catalogue

Every parameter of every P1a module kind, as declared in `crates/enth-patch/src/catalogue/` (lane S1a, freeze point F1, 2026-10-19). Values are plain numbers in the unit shown; patches store them densely in this order. **The source of truth is the code and its snapshot** (`crates/enth-patch/tests/snapshots/catalogue__catalogue.snap`); this page is generated from it by `cargo test -p enth-patch --test catalogue -- --ignored --nocapture print_markdown` and may lag behind. S2a's eleven reserved kinds are hidden, so their tables are not here: each is in its own `docs/dsp/<kind>.md`, "Parameters" (generated by `print_reserved_markdown`), and the decisions behind them are [below](#s2a-decisions-lane-cat-f1m).

How to read it:

- **Range** is in plain units; `enth` parses `800Hz`, `1.2kHz`, `A4`, `40ms`, `0.5s`, `-6dB`, `+7st`, `12ct`, `+1oct`, `30%`, `4:1`, `90deg`, enum keys and `on`/`off`, and a bare number is in the parameter's unit (`30` on a percent parameter is 30 %).
- **Scale** maps the range onto the normalised 0–1 domain used by route depths, the randomiser, ML and host automation. `log` gives every octave the same share (10.034 % of 20 Hz–20 kHz); `power k` is `min + (max − min)·n^k` (power 4 puts 625 ms at the middle of 0–10 s). The scale is part of the persisted contract: changing it needs a migration.
- **Flags:** `mod` = modulation routes may target it; `auto` = an automation slot may expose it to the host; `structural` = changing it rebuilds the render plan (never modulated or automated); `periodic` = wraps instead of clamping.
- Route **depth** is a percentage of the destination's range in its normalised domain, −100..100; polarity belongs to the source (an LFO's `polarity`; envelopes, velocity, mod wheel and macros run 0–1; `key` is `(note − 60)/120`).
- Sources: any `adsr` or `lfo` module, `velocity`, `key`, `modwheel`, `macro1`…`macro8`. A per-voice source (an envelope, a voice LFO, velocity, key) may not target a global destination (`fx` modules, `master`, a global LFO).
- `phase_mode` (osc, lfo): `free` draws each note's start phase from the render seed (the descriptors' noise estimate varies it), `reset` starts at `phase`, `seeded` draws it from the module's own `seed`, so the patch fixes it.
- Limits: 4 generators, 4 voice effects, 4 effects, 8 modulators, 64 routes, 8 macros, 64 automation slots, polyphony ≤ 16.

### The five decisions phase-s2.md gives CAT

1. **The MSEG has 16 points.** Each is a time (a share of the length), a level and a curve for the segment leaving it; `points` (2–16, default 3) says how many make the shape, so simple shapes stay simple and adding a point changes nothing until it moves. Why 16: report 04 asks for 8–16 points on a 1/16 grid, and a curve of ±100 % is a step, so 16 points over one bar are a 16-step pattern (riddim gates, chopped volume shapes) where 8 would stop at eighths. 16 × 3 = 48, plus 11 others (`mode`, `sync`, `length`, `beats`, `polarity`, `scope`, `phase`, `velocity`, `points`, `loop_start`, `loop_end`) is 59, within the answer code's 63 with 4 to spare. **The length** is `beats` of the tempo when `sync` is on (default on, 1 bar) or `length` in ms (10 ms–60 s); `beats` is the shared `Beats` enum (`catalogue/mod.rs`, the coordinator's `92c31ff`, used by `lfo` too). **Modes:** `loop`, `one_shot`, `sustain_loop` (loops `loop_start`…`loop_end` while the note is held; equal points hold a level). **`scope`** (voice or global, structural, as `lfo`'s) lets a global MSEG modulate effects; lane MSEG wires it (an `mseg` arm in `ModuleKind::rate` and a global modulator arm in the engine), and until then an MSEG runs per voice.
2. **Envelope times above 10 s are the MSEG**, not a new `adsr` parameter and not another kind. A one-shot or sustain-loop MSEG runs up to 60 s free or 16 bars synced (an 8-bar riser at 140 BPM is 13.7 s; report 04's foghorn decay is 17 s), with curves, so it is the long envelope, and synced it is exact in bars. An `adsr` parameter was ruled out: every patch has an `adsr` (the amplitude envelope), so a new one rehashes every patch's render-cache key, moves the main catalogue snapshot and `adsr`'s answer-code row, and it would need building and a bit-exact default on a visible kind; `adsr`'s ranges cannot move (layout 1 reads them). **The limit:** `voice.amp_env` must name an `adsr` (validation rule 5), so an amplitude envelope keeps `adsr`'s 10 s attack and decay and 20 s release; S9's 17 s amplitude decay is an `adsr` at sustain 100 % plus a one-shot MSEG on the generators' levels. Letting an MSEG be the amplitude envelope (validation and the engine's voice end) is an S2b option.
3. **`wt`'s unison parameters are in F1m's table now** (`unison` 1–8, `detune`, `blend`, `spread`), inert until lane UNI and hidden with the kind. The judge reviews every persisted `wt` key at once, the editor (EDS), templates (TPL) and the G2 patch (G2B) plan against the final card, and the count is known (18 of 63). `unison` is **structural**, so whether a plan needs lane SVP's stereo voice path follows from the patch alone (a structural edit recompiles; a modulated or automated value cannot); the recommendation to UNI is stereo when an enabled `wt` has `unison` ≥ 2, so `spread` stays modulatable and `spread` 0 equals mono. 8 copies fill one `f32x8` lane, so the CPU bound is one lane per voice.
4. **What `lfo` gains** is lane TMP's (`sync`, `beats`, `one_shot`, in the table above and in [lfo.md](lfo.md#tempo-sync-and-one-shot-s2a-lane-tmp)); CAT added nothing to `lfo`. `mseg`, `chorus`, `phaser` and `delay` use the same shared `Beats` enum; `delay` offers its prefix up to one bar (`DELAY_BEATS`, the same keys and indices), since its rings hold 2 s.
5. **OTT's defaults.** Used: crossovers at **88.3 Hz and 2.5 kHz** (report 04, from two blogs: Bedroom Producers Blog and Mixelite); `upward` and `downward` 100 % and `time` 100 % (OTT's own knobs at their reference); `input`, `output` and the band gains 0 dB; and **`depth` 30 %**, deliberately not the full effect, for the neutral-default rule. **Confidence: low to medium.** A web search on 2026-10-26 found no primary source for the preset's values (Mixelite and Slate's MO-TT page describe the knobs, not the numbers). The bands' thresholds, ratios and attack and release times are lane OTT's constants, not catalogue parameters, so lane OTT should verify them by reading Ableton's preset or measuring Xfer OTT (a download needs the owner's OK) without moving a key. `depth` is intensity (like Ableton's Amount), not Xfer's dry/wet Depth: a dry blend of a band-split signal notches the crossovers, and `mix` exists for the blend anyway.

### Other judgment calls

- **`wt.pm` and `wt.am` keep S0d's keys and flags** (audio, not modulatable: lane RTE's contract). Their values are each input's base amount. **PM:** phase offset = `pm_index · (pm / 100) · pm_in(t)`, `pm_in` the summed routed input (each source pre-level × route depth); `pm` is a trim (default 100 %), and the new **`pm_index`** (0–10 rad, modulatable) is the amount, because no control-rate source can reach an audio parameter and the buffers are pre-level, so without it nothing could make an FM growl talk (report 04's S4 routes an MSEG to the index). **AM:** gain = `am / 100 + am_in(t)`: 100 % with no route is unity, a route makes AM with sidebands at `depth / (2·am)`, and `am` 0 % with a route is ring modulation (no carrier), which meets WT's spectral checks (phase-s2.md, WT's row).
- **`formant` takes two formant frequencies (`f1`, `f2`, in Hz), not one vowel knob.** No path through the vowel plane makes report 04's moves: a "yoi" swings F2 from 2.4 kHz to 640 Hz while F1 stays at 240–360 Hz, and a growl's "a → o" lowers both; the A-E-I-O-U path passes "e" and "i" between "a" and "o", a back-to-front path passes "a" between "i" and "o". Two Hz knobs place every vowel, report 04's vowel table is the settings, and a vowel path is one modulator routed to both at two depths.
- **Effects whose sound contains the dry signal** (`comb`, `chorus`, `phaser`, `delay`) define their 100 % with it, so the rule's default `mix` 100 % is the full chorus, phaser or echo and never a dry-less wet signal (a fully wet chorus is a vibrato; a fully wet delay loses the note). For them `mix` scales the wet part against an unchanged dry signal.
- **`dist.keep_lows`** (the band below it bypasses the curve; off at 20 Hz) is report 04's fallback for the sub lane, which S2a does not build: report 04 marks a clean sub essential for ten of its fourteen families. **`dist.oversample`** is structural (a factor change rebuilds the plan instead of clicking), default 2×. Downsampling is `rate`, bitcrushing `bits`; report 04's S6 "MSEG on drive" in downsample mode is an MSEG on `rate` here.
- **`ladder` mirrors `svf`** (same keys, units, ranges and defaults where they mean the same) plus a `mode` from the stage taps, so a patch can swap one for the other.
- **`eq` has six fixed bands** (low cut, low shelf, two bells, high shelf, high cut), the cuts off at the ends of their ranges, which covers report 04's EQ moves (a low cut with a bell, a modulated high shelf, a bell with a shelf).
- **Order of new enums:** curves by family (clip, fold, digital) for `dist.mode`; `Beats` by length (the coordinator's); `wt.table` from basic to exotic, with `basic` first because its first frame is the sine an FM operator wants; `wt.phase_mode` defaults to `reset`, since PM, warps and feedback make the start phase part of the timbre.
- **Modulatable:** continuous controls that move in performance; times read at segment starts (`dist`'s oversampling, `ott.time`, `delay.time`), counts and enums are not. `chorus`, `phaser`, `delay`, `eq` and `ott` are global, so only global sources (a global `lfo` or `mseg`, macros, the mod wheel) reach them, as for `reverb` and `comp`.
