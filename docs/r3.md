# Sound → patch (R3): create from sound, then a model's proposals

R3 turns a sound file into editable patches, fully locally: "Create from sound" in the CLI and the plugin. It ships in two steps. **R3a** has no ML: a robust intake, a degradation-aware objective, a portfolio of starts with k-NN retrieval, and K branches refined by search on a cancellable pool. **R3b** adds a small model whose K hypotheses become extra starts, run on our own scalar kernels. ## Why R3 starts with search, and with the intake

- **Nobody turns arbitrary real sounds into patches well** (report 01). All published work trains on the synth's own renders, and out-of-domain error roughly doubles (Hayes 2025: spectral error 6.1 in-domain against 11.0 on NSynth and 15.4 on FSD50K). Nobody has tested degraded uploads. The one comparison of model + search with search alone is small and old: a warm start matched a GA in 40 % of its time (Shier 2021, 7 parameters). Synplant 2 publishes no method.
- **Today's search copies the damage** (report 04 §2, *measured*: `enth match` on one target, 400 renders, scored against the **clean** target):

  | Input | Sound % vs clean | What the search did |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | Clean | 98.98 (pass) | cutoff 6.90 kHz |
  | MP3 64 kbps | 91.92 (pass) | cutoff 4.58 kHz, copying the codec's low-pass |
  | Noise, 20 dB SNR | **10.27** | cutoff 58 Hz, release 4.46 s |
  | The same, tail trimmed to 1.8 s | 55.98 | cutoff 8.21 kHz, release 720 ms (truth 300) |
  | Reverb, RT60 1.2 s | **24.87** | release 1.56 s |
  | Clipped after +12 dB | **64.35** | cutoff 51 Hz, drive 6.2 dB, sustain 84 % |

  CMA-ES turns knobs to reproduce noise, reverb and clipping, and in model + search it would undo what an augmentation-trained model learned. One target and one seed is an existence proof, not a rate; lane OBJ repeats it on 40 targets × 4 degradations. So **R3a needs a robust intake and a degradation-aware objective before it ships**, and GR3 judges degraded uploads against the clean original.

## R3a: create from sound with search alone

### The intake (lane FE)

**Built (2026-10-21, lane FE); reviewed at F1s with TP's `create` API, and lane F1S closed the judge's findings** (the floor's provenance, square waves, the onset on the render grid, the report's settings, the spurious phrase). Pure functions in `enth-analysis/src/intake/` (`trim`, `floor`, `bandwidth`, `clip`, `perf`). They are wasm32-clean and run on the scalar FFT path. They describe what an upload still carries, so the search stops paying knobs to reproduce damage. **Short sounds are never rejected**, and nothing is filtered by duration.

**The entry point and the seam with TP** (agreed through the coordinator, 2026-10-21):

```rust
let it = Intake::new(&interleaved, channels, sample_rate)?;   // or Intake::with_cfg(.., &IntakeCfg)
it.audio                // Vec<f32>: mono, the file's own rate, the onset at report.perf.on_s, at most 5 s
it.report               // IntakeReport: serde, version 1 (with its IntakeCfg); enth.create/1 embeds it whole
let target = create::Target::from_intake(&it, enth_render::DEFAULT_SEED);   // scored over the first 3 s (create::SCORE_S)
```

| What `create` takes | Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The clip | `audio` | The mean of the channels, cut and capped. A clip under 0.1 s is padded with zeros. **The onset is at `perf.on_s`, not at sample 0:** a pre-roll of about 5 ms, rounded up to whole 32-frame engine segments, so a note played at `on_s` starts exactly on the render grid. Zeros are prepended when the file starts sooner. The onset itself sits on the file's own grid, so an engine render comes back sample for sample (below) |
| The rate | `report.sample_rate` | The file's own rate, 8,000 to 191,999 Hz (the analysis's range). No resampling |
| What to play | `report.perf`: `key`, `velocity`, `on_s`, `hold_s`, `duration_s`, `f0_hz`, `off_s` | One note. TP builds the `Performance` (`enth-render` is the same layer as `enth-analysis`). `velocity` is 100/127, the CLI's default, because the metric normalises loudness. `hold_s` runs to the note-off, or to where the signal falls into the floor when the sound only decays |
| The band | `report.degradation.bandwidth_hz` | A steep low-pass's cut-off (Hz), or `None` for the full band; mask above `min(bandwidth_hz, sample_rate / 2)` |
| The floor | `report.degradation.noise_floor_dbfs` | RMS dBFS of the clip as delivered; `None` when there is no noise-only stretch. With `floor_source` (`lead_in`, `quiet`, `both`) and `floor_confirmed`: **use it only when confirmed** (a held noise-like sound reads as an unconfirmed floor), and `Target::from_intake` passes only a confirmed one. For OBJ and `enth.create/1`; the search ignores it |

Also in the report, for the CLI, the plugin and OBJ:

- `cfg`: the `IntakeCfg` it ran with;
- `degradation.snr_db` and `hum` (50 or 60 Hz), both from a confirmed floor only, `clipping` (hard-clipped spans, clip time), `tail_t60_s` (the final decay: a release and a room tail look alike here);
- `perf.onsets_s` and `perf.voiced`;
- `span` (where the clip came from, in source seconds);
- `warnings`: `phrase`, `notes`, `chord`, `unpitched`, `capped`, `channels_cancel` (a kind a newer build adds reads back as `unknown`).

Errors: `Empty`, `NoChannels`, `SampleRate`, `NonFinite`, `Silent` (peak under −100 dBFS), `BadConfig`. Never a short sound.

**How each piece decides** (the module docs have the full rules):

- **trim:**
  - 30 s of the file are looked at, from a second before its first sound, so the work is bounded for any length;
  - the onset is the first 20 ms frame at or above the threshold, narrowed to a 1 ms window, then to the sample;
  - the threshold is 60 dB under the loudest frame, or 10–20 dB above the floor (the margin grows with the floor's spread);
  - a pre-echo guard moves the onset to the first window within 25 dB of the level reached in the next 15 ms, which undoes an MP3's smear;
  - the onset is then **placed on the file's render grid**: back to just after the last digitally silent sample within 4 ms (the first sample of a sound that starts from silence), then down to a whole number of 32-frame segments from the file's first sample, so an engine render (whose grid starts at its first frame) comes back with its note-on frame as the onset, whatever its attack; anything else moves earlier by less than a segment plus 4 ms;
  - the tail is cut 100 ms after the signal falls into the floor;
- **floor:**
  - read from two stretches: the quietest one (the frames within 6 dB of the 10th percentile) and the lead-in (the frames before the level first rises 6 dB);
  - a stretch counts only if it is level (20 dB/s at most), not a note (YIN finds nothing but the mains), and 6–70 dB under the peak;
  - a file holding 100 ms of digital silence is clean;
  - **only a confirmed floor trims** (the F1s judge's M4: a held noise-like sound's quietest stretch passes every check above, and a 3 s noise held at a 10–40 % sustain was cut to its 0.15–0.21 s attack). It is confirmed by the lead-in (within 6 dB of the reported level), or, with no lead-in, when the sound's loudest 300 ms are a note and the quiet stretch is not that note (no pitch, or another pitch class such as the mains). Only a confirmed floor moves the trim's thresholds, the tail's measurement, the pitch and onset gates and the hum; an unconfirmed one is reported and flagged, and the clip is trimmed at the peak less `silence_db`, as a clean file is;
- **bandwidth:**
  - the highest edge from 2 to 16 kHz with a drop of at least 20 dB within 1/3 octave that stays down;
  - the octave below it must be full (three partials or more within 35 dB) and have no resonant bump (6 dB at most);
  - edges near Nyquist are left out, where a render's own filters fall;
- **clip:** runs of at least 5 samples at the clip's own extreme, except a square wave's flat tops: a plateau jumped onto and off from the other extreme within 3 samples (a band-limited step); when those are half an extreme's plateaus, the extreme is a square's, envelope edges included;
- **perf:**
  - **pitch:** the YIN median over 80 ms frames (27.5 Hz to 4.5 kHz), with `pitch::tonal_pitch` on the held part's prominent peaks when YIN finds none;
  - **note-off:** from `infer_note_off`;
  - **onsets:** Böck and Widmer's super flux, plus a 2 dB rise in level, in frames the clip holds whole (a frame cut by the clip's end splatters like an onset: the held pad's spurious phrase at C5);
  - **chord:** either an unrelated second harmonic series, or a virtual pitch with its first two harmonics missing (a major triad reads as harmonics 4, 5 and 6).

The "16/120 clean renders with clipping" of FE's summary are not false alarms: the 24-bit writer clamped the 20 renders whose float peak was over 0 dBFS, and 16 of those plateaus are found. With the strict rule alone (only the lead-in confirms), noisy files with a lead-in under 50–60 ms lost their onset (noise 101/120, band + bit 116/120); the note route above restores them without trusting a held sound's sustain.

**Decisions for the F1s judge** (the judge endorsed 1–6 and 8; 7 in scope, but not as shipped, since square waves read as clipped: fixed by F1S):

1. **`resample.rs` is deleted.** Render and analysis run at the file's own rate, the model's features put their bands in Hz, and nothing in the intake needs a resampler.
2. **The onset sits at `on_s`,** a pre-roll of whole 32-frame segments, rather than at sample 0, so the attack is never cut and the candidate's note-on lands exactly on the grid. The intake knows the segment length as a documented constant (`trim::SEGMENT_FRAMES`).
3. **The velocity is not inferred** (a fixed 100/127).
4. **A short clip is padded with zeros to 0.1 s** rather than rejected. Below that, the analysis refuses (`Describer` needs 20 ms).
5. **The sample-rate range is the analysis's:** 192 kHz exactly is refused, as `Describer` and the CLI refuse it.
6. **`bandwidth_hz` is reported only up to 16 kHz:** higher cuts cost the search nothing audible, and a render's own filters fall steeply near Nyquist.
7. **Only hard clipping is detected.** Soft clipping and limiting leave no plateau, and a codec rings over one. OBJ should not rely on `clipping` for those.
8. **`tail_t60_s` is the final decay of whatever is last.** It is not a reverb detector: the policy's dry and wet candidates (above) handle rooms.

**Limits and open items:**

- An unpitched sound (noise, a bell, a snare) that starts at the file's first sample and decays into real noise has no confirmed floor, so it keeps its noisy tail up to the length cap (the price of never cutting a held sound to its attack); the floor is still reported, unconfirmed, for OBJ.
- Content above about 1.2 kHz clipped by 12 dB or more at 48 kHz looks like a square and is not reported as clipped.
- Slow attacks under noise surface 5–35 ms late (F1s should-fix 6). **Done in the objective** by lane OBJ (`robust::attack_lead`, [below](#what-lane-obj-built-2026-10-21)); the trim's own onset stays where it is (OBJ decision 6), so the Create view's audition plays a candidate at the intake's onset, up to about 35 ms after such an upload's attack (INTa's status, 2026-10-22).
- Sparse high notes (f0 above about 1 kHz) hide a codec's cut (should-fix 2). **Done** by lane SK3: the decoder's codec and bitrate hint in `IntakeCfg::codec` ([plugin.md](plugin.md#decoders)).
- 192 kHz files have no path (should-fix 10). **Done** by lane SK3: files at 192 kHz and above are decimated exactly 2:1 in the decoder ([plugin.md](plugin.md#decoders)).
- The report is checked by an insta fixture of its shape; its JSON read-back is `tools/datagen/tests/create_record.rs` (the record embeds it whole), since `enth-analysis` has no JSON reader.

### The robust objective (lane OBJ)

A degradation-aware distance in `enth-analysis`, under **a new metric id** that OBJ names; `sound/1` and every score already pinned stay unchanged. Its intent: compare a candidate with the upload only where the intake says the upload still carries information (the trimmed span, the band below the detected cut-off, levels above the noise floor, unclipped frames), so the search stops paying knobs to reproduce damage. OBJ designs it and proves it: on 40 dev targets × 4 degradations, the median `sound/1` against the clean target improves on v1.1, and clean inputs stay within 1 point.

**The degradation policy** (yours to decide; the default): codec damage, hum, noise and device EQ count as damage to ignore; for room reverb and compression, which the patch can also make, the K candidates include a dry patch and one using the patch's own reverb or compressor, and you choose by ear.

#### What lane OBJ built (2026-10-21)

**`logmel-pool85-robust/1`** (`enth_analysis::robust::METRIC_ROBUST`): metric v1.1 (`logmel-pool85-l1/1`, the 85 ms pooled log-mel L1 at −23 LUFS) with four changes, each driven by what FE's `IntakeReport` says (`robust::Damage::from_report`). With no damage it **equals v1.1 bit for bit** (a test pins it).

| Damage | What the distance does | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A codec's or a phone's low-pass | Compares only the mel bands whose upper edge is at or below `min(bandwidth_hz, sr / 2)`, and level-aligns the candidate over them (half the log ratio of their summed squared magnitudes), so the energy above the cut neither shifts the kept bands nor rewards imitating the cut | `bandwidth_hz` found |
| Noise and hum | Reads the noise off the clip itself, per resolution and band: the quietest pooled block, plus the known spread of a stationary noise's pooled level (`NOISE_SPREAD` 0.7 / √(bins × frames), measured on white and pink noise). The candidate is compared **as if recorded in the same noise** (the noise's mean, quietest + 2 spreads, added in power), then both are clamped at the floor (quietest + 4 spreads). A candidate that adds noise of its own (a noise module, a long release, a reverb tail) is charged twice; what lies under the noise is neither paid nor charged. Hum is stationary, so its harmonics are in the floor | **a confirmed floor only** (FE decision 7) |
| Hard clipping | Nothing: weighting the blocks that overlap a clipped span down (0.25) changed nothing on the dev uploads (below), so they are compared like the rest; `Damage` keeps the spans and `Settings::clip_weight` stays for an intake that finds more | (soft clipping and limiting are never inferred from `clipping`, FE decision 7) |
| The silence after the sound | When the clip ends because the sound fell into the floor (not capped, not cut by the file or the scored window), the candidate is rendered 0.5 s longer (within `SCORE_S`) and its extra blocks are compared, clamped, with what the upload holds there: the confirmed floor, or silence (60 dB under the clip's loudest cell). v1.1 stopped looking 100 ms after the signal's end, so a candidate that rang on was free | the clip ends in its floor |
| A slow attack that surfaced late | `robust::attack_lead` fits a line to the amplitude (floor power subtracted) of 10 ms frames every 5 ms over the rise and reads where it meets zero; the candidate's note starts that much earlier (at most 50 ms; the render starts whole segments early when that is before the clip). On FE's lags it reads 18.5 for 18 ms, 20.2 for 24, 28.8 for 22, 8.6 for 6 and 9.8 for 6.7; toy and sine ramps within about 1 ms. This is F1s should-fix 6, done in the objective rather than the trim | a confirmed floor only |

**The seam with the shells** (TP's API unchanged): `enth_solver::robust::RobustMetricTarget::from_intake(&target, &intake.report)` beside metric v1's `MetricTarget`, one `RobustWorker` per thread (the `Objective` a shell's pool wraps, as it wraps `MetricWorker`), and `METRIC_ROBUST` in `enth.create/1`'s `settings`. `from_target(&Target)` uses what `Target` carries (the bandwidth and a confirmed floor); `with_damage` takes an explicit `Damage`. A worker renders with the target's performance, longer for the silence after the sound and earlier for a late attack, and returns `FAILED` for a patch that does not render; a create job under it is the same for any order of work (tested).

**Against the acceptance:** the median over the 160 degraded uploads rises from 76.0 to 79.7, and on the two kinds of damage the distance treats, from 77.4 to 87.0 (MP3) and from 58.9 to 74.6 (noise and hum); clean uploads rise 1.6 points (within 1 point either way is the bar). `reverb` and `clip-limit` are flat (−1.4 and +0.8 on the median, paired medians 0): by the policy neither is treated as damage (below), and the train pool's `clip-limit` is mostly limiting and tanh clipping, which leave no plateau to find.

**What each part buys** (the same runs with one part off, on the uploads where it acts; on clean uploads the distance without the silence after the sound searched exactly as v1.1 on 39 of 40):

| Part | Uploads where it acts | v1.1 | Without the part | With it | Paired Δ, median (mean) | Better / worse |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The silence after the sound | clean, 40 | 88.8 | 88.8 | 90.4 | 0.0 (+4.0) | 14 / 13 |
| The band mask (with the alignment) | MP3 with a cut found, 31 | 70.4 | 79.8 | 86.8 | +1.5 (+6.2) | 16 / 8 |
| Clipped blocks at 0.25 (not kept) | clip-limit with clipping found, 19 | 79.1 | 79.7 (weight 1) | 78.2 (weight 0.25) | 0.0 (0.0) | 6 / 8 |
| The late attack's lead | noisy, with a lead read, 6 | 54.8 | 85.1 | 84.4 | 0.0 (−0.2) | 2 / 1 |

The silence after the sound is what turns a few failures around (two clean targets from 73 and 60 to 95 and 82, a reverberant one from 5 to 76: candidates that rang on past the upload's end); on MP3 uploads it carries most of the gain on its own (70.4 → 79.8), and the mask the rest. The lead does not move `sound/1` (whose headline barely sees attack times, ADR-0007's note), but it is what puts the attack right: on those 6 uploads the fitted amplitude attack is off from the truth by a median factor of e^1.18 (a third of it) under v1.1, e^0.31 under the robust distance without the lead, and e^0.06 (6 %) with it (velvet pad b: 120, 1,148 and 724 ms for 700). The floor's two halves were settled on the noisy target where the clamp alone failed (decision 4) rather than by a 35-run ablation, for time.

**How it meets the degradation policy.** Noise, hum and codec damage are ignored as the policy asks (device EQ has no detector yet: an EQ'd upload is compared as clean). Room reverb and compression are **not** treated as damage: the distance has no reliable detector for either (`tail_t60_s` is the decay of whatever is last, and limiting leaves no plateau), so it compares them as part of the sound, and a candidate with the patch's own reverb or compressor is scored fairly against a wet upload. The dry candidate the policy asks for is then the best of the K without a reverb (the table's "dry" column); the silence-after-the-sound term starts after the room tail, so it does not punish a wet candidate for the tail the upload has. What it cannot do is make the top candidate dry on a reverberant upload: that is the portfolio's job (a dry and a wet start among the K, TP's) and the user's choice by ear.

**Decisions** (provisional, for the review):

1. **The id is `logmel-pool85-robust/1`**, a metric id beside `logmel-l1/1` and `logmel-pool85-l1/1`, not a scorer id: `sound/1` stays the judge and the game's scorer.
2. **v1.1 underneath, not v1** (today's create objective is v1's `MetricWorker`): v1.1 is what `sound/1` uses and what the acceptance compares with, and its pooling cuts the render-to-render nuisance floor 2–20× (ADR-0007's note).
3. **The noise is read off the clip, not from the intake's dBFS level.** A scalar level cannot give a band shape; a correction of the floor to that level (when the clip holds no noise-only block) was tried and dropped: it shifted the floor of every band, the noise-only ones included, about a neper under the noise, and the search went back to copying it (one target: the exact patch 0.48, a wrong one 0.13, `sound/1` 49.5 against 99.9 once removed).
4. **Clamping alone was not enough:** the upload reads the *sum* of sound and noise wherever they overlap, which a clean candidate falls short of, so a candidate with its own noise still won (toy: the clean pluck 0.060 against the same pluck with another noise 0.006). Adding the noise's mean to the candidate fixes it (0.057 against 0.124).
5. **The silence after the sound costs renders:** a candidate renders up to 0.5 s longer when the clip ends inside the window (a 1.8 s clip: about a quarter more per evaluation). It is kept: it costs no target anything on the median and rescues the ringing failures above.
6. **The late attack is corrected in the objective**, not in FE's trim: the trim stays a pure function of the file, and the lead needs the noise floor and the attack's shape, which only matter when comparing.
7. **No hum-specific weighting:** hum is stationary, so the floor holds it. A notch on the mains bands would blind the lowest bands where bass notes live.

**Open items:**

- **The default budget (4,000) was not measured** (1,000 here); EVK's arm-A runs will show whether the gains hold with more search.
- **The search's own spread:** one seed per run, so single items swing by tens of points (octave-ambiguous candidates, wet against dry); the medians over 40 are the measure, and the per-item list is in the scratch runs.
- **Device EQ** (a phone's high-pass, peaks) has no detector in the intake; an EQ'd upload is compared as clean.
- **Limiting and soft clipping** stay sound, per FE decision 7 and the policy (compression): they need the portfolio's compressed start, not the objective.
- **Held sounds under noise:** a clip with no noise-only block reads the sound's quietest part as its floor, so the comparison is blind within about 4 spreads (3–7 dB) of each band's quietest block.
- `sound/1` has no JND scale for real recordings (r3.md risks), so real uploads are judged by ear; this measurement is on synth targets only.

### The start portfolio and k-NN retrieval (lane TP)

Every create job starts from several places at once:

- **the heuristic start**, always kept ([dsp/match.md](dsp/match.md#the-heuristic-heuristicrs): pitch → tuning, centroid and tilt → filter, envelope → ADSR, noisiness → noise);
- **the templates** (`content/templates/`: `init`, `pluck_1osc`, `pad_2osc`, `bass_sub`), because structure is never searched otherwise (GR1's level 5: 0 of 4);
- **k-NN retrieval** over a pre-rendered patch bank (report 01 §7: 50–200 k generator patches, matched on a PCA'd pooled log-mel, later on the model's own embedding). TP fills the `nearest.rs` stub with it, which keeps that stub's meaning ("the nearest preset as a start"). Retrieval needs no training, gives several candidates, and is the baseline every learned model must beat. **The bank's size, features and delivery are TP's open check;** the default is seeds plus f16 vectors in a versioned file beside the plugin, regenerated by an xtask command, and R3a runs without it.

### K branches and successive halving

- Each candidate is its own `Search` (the existing ask/tell CMA-ES, which already takes one start and has `cancel()`). Each round merges every live search's `ask()` batch into one pool batch and splits the distances back to each `tell()`, so **the outcome does not depend on the worker count**; 8 searches × λ 16 = 128 jobs a round keep a pool busy.
- **Successive halving** is deterministic and **independent of the budget** (the coordinator's decision at F1s): each branch has milestones at m0 · 2^r of its own renders (m0 = 50, tuned on dev); at each the best half survive (at least the two finalists), and the finalists continue in doubling rungs until the cap. The budget is only a hard cap, so a capped run is an exact prefix of a longer one: one long run gives the whole anytime curve, a 300 s run contains the 60 s run, and a timed run's twin is just its renders as the budget.
- **Scale:** Phase 0's toy synth gave about 600 evaluations/s on 8 workers (about 36,000 in 60 s); GR1's floors suggest about 200 renders/s on 4 workers for R1's engine (12,000 in 60 s). TP measures the per-evaluation cost with effects before K and the budget are fixed.
- **Time:** the solver reads no clock, so the wall-clock cap lives in the shell's `search_with` callback (as `--max-seconds` does): it stops at the cap minus the intake. The front end (decode, trim, features) aims at 0.2 s, inference (R3b) at 1 s, and the rest of a 60 s target goes to search; **the first candidate arrives within 15 s**, and a result never takes more than 5 minutes.

**The schedule** (`create/schedule.rs`):

1. **Round 0 screens every start once** (at most the budget's worth), so K proposals have distances after one batch, well inside the 15 s to the first candidate. The heuristic start (the init template with the heuristic already applied, so its proposal is the heuristic's) is always kept; the best K − 1 others become the branches, in start order.
2. **Each branch is its own `Search`** from its start over what the held phrase reveals (`observable`, as `enth match`'s default) less the envelopes' release times when the target's note-off falls at or after the clip's end, with the target's heuristic and a seed derived from the job's seed and the start's index. A branch's search has no render cap of its own, and its first batch reuses its start's screening distance instead of rendering the start again.
3. **Each round** concatenates every eligible branch's `ask()` in branch order and splits the distances back by index.
4. **Successive halving on each branch's renders, independent of the budget** (the F1s judge's M2; the coordinator's decision): rung r ends at the milestone `first_rung · eta^r` of a branch's own renders (50, 100, 200, … by default); a branch stops asking once it has reached it (a batch boundary) and waits; when every live branch has reached it or ended, the best ⌈live / eta⌉ survive, at least the finalists. From 8 branches, 4 survive their 50th render and 2 their 100th, and those 2 carry on through the doubling rungs until the cap.
5. **The budget is only a hard cap:** a round that would pass it keeps the whole batches that fit, in branch order up to the first that does not, and is the last. Nothing else depends on the budget, and nothing on time.
6. **Hence a capped run is an exact prefix of a longer one:** the patches a run capped by its budget evaluates, in order, are a longer run's first ones, and a run the shell's clock stopped (the progress callback returning `false`) is the same job with its renders as the budget, round for round and candidate for candidate. A shell sets the budget it allows (or one it will not reach) and stops by the clock; the record replays either.

Tests (`crates/enth-solver/tests/`): `create.rs` (TP): K branches and identical results for 1 and 8 workers (real renders through a thread pool); a slow, uneven objective and callback change nothing, and a cap after round 5 is a prefix of the full run; every branch ends each rung on the batch that took it to its milestone, the live branches go 7, 4, 2, and every budget tried is respected; the heuristic start carries the heuristic and survives a screening it loses; a record replays the run from its starts alone; a 10 ms clip is kept. `create_replay.rs` (F1S, the judge's probes as regressions): a run capped at budgets from 2 to 2,222 evaluates exactly a 3,000-render run's first patches, each start is rendered once, the twin of a run stopped after 1–30 rounds is the same job with its renders as the budget, and a record replays a run the clock stopped under the settings it ran under. `create_intake.rs` (F1S): `trim::SEGMENT_FRAMES` is the engine's segment; an engine render through the intake and `from_intake`, rendered again, matches sample for sample to the note-off (48, 44.1 and 22.05 kHz; 4 and 120 ms attacks); the checklist's mapping and the 3 s window; the judge's FE probes. **k-NN retrieval** (`nearest.rs`, embedding `knn-logmel-rel/1`):

- **The embedding:** metric v1's log-mel (−23 LUFS, 1024-point resolution), pooled over four segments from the note-on (0–0.1, 0.1–0.3, 0.3–0.8, 0.8–2 s; a segment past the clip's end reads as the floor, so short sounds stay valid), read at 28 third-octave bands **relative to the measured pitch** (half the pitch up to 256 times it; a fixed C3 for unpitched clips, so noise keeps absolute bands). Bands below 30 Hz or above 16 kHz, half the rate or **the intake's detected bandwidth** are masked. One byte per band (1/16 neper ≈ 0.54 dB; code 0 is masked): 112 bytes.
- **Why pitch-relative:** the bank renders each patch once, at C3; a query at any note matches by harmonic shape, and the neighbour is transposed by its measured pitch offset (whole semitones on every oscillator) before the search's heuristic fixes the cents. A PCA was not needed at 112 bytes, and masking needs the bands.
- **The distance** is the mean absolute difference over the bands both carry, computed on the bytes in integers and compared by cross-multiplication: the ranking is the same on every platform for the same codes; ties go to the lower index. Neighbours closer than `min_spread` to one already taken are skipped, so the starts differ.
- **Does it help?** `cargo xtask bank probe`: 100 held-aside generator targets (the bank's stream far past any entry, levels 2–5, played at G3 for 1.5 s, not at the bank's C3), metric v1 of each start **before any search**:

  | the best of the 4 templates | 0.921 | 0.921 | 0.921 |
  | 8 random bank entries, the best | 0.845 | 0.944 | 0.873 |
  | the nearest neighbour | 0.935 | 0.890 | 0.890 |
  | the best of the 8 nearest (spread 0.25) | 0.615 | 0.660 | 0.643 |

  Retrieval is worth its screening renders, but only as a set: the nearest neighbour alone is no better than a template, and the screening round picks among the neighbours, so `neighbours` defaults to 16 (20 screening renders with the templates, 0.5 % of the budget). A bank ten times larger does not do better on these targets: the embedding's resolution, not the bank's size, is the limit. ### The cancellable pool (lane SK3)

The plugin's `enth-game` worker is joined on drop ("a few hundred ms at most"), so a 60 s job there would hang an unload. Create jobs get their own:

1. **an `enth-create` orchestrator thread**, spawned at the first job and joined on drop, like `enth-game`: it decodes, computes features, runs inference on one thread (milliseconds) and drives the searches. It never runs on the audio thread and never holds the session lock while computing; results are posted the way the game worker posts them;
2. **a per-job `rayon::ThreadPool`** (never the global one), named `enth-search-N` and dropped when the job ends, so no threads exist between jobs; workers `min(available_parallelism / 2, 8)` at normal priority, never real-time;
3. **cancel:** an `AtomicBool` checked per job in the pool and after each round, so a cancel lands within one batch (about one evaluation, ~10 ms);
4. **gated under load:** `enth-test-host process-stats --gate g1` with a create job running, as lane SKR gated the A/B, because isolated benchmarks mislead under DAW load.

## R3b: the first model

### `ds1`: training data from our engine (lane DG)

Renders are bit-exact, so a dataset is a manifest (a sampler version, seeds, a phrase sampler, an augmentation config) and clean audio never needs shipping or backing up. **Generator v1 is unfit as it stands** (level 1 has about 12 sounds, every phrase starts on C3 with holds of at least 1.5 s, `amounts` below 100 pull values towards the start), so a new versioned sampler, `ds1`, reuses v1's keyed draws, priors and `observable()` mask and adds:

- **a mixture** (a starting point to tune on dev): 60 % v1 priors at `amounts` 100 and level 5, structure included, with `musical` drawn from {100, 50, 0} at 0.6 / 0.25 / 0.15; 25 % perturbed preset candidates (σ 0.05–0.2 in the normalised domain plus a few categorical flips), **from the anchor half only**; 15 % uniform over the safe ranges;
- **a phrase sampler:** key log-weighted over MIDI 24–84, velocity 30–127, hold log-uniform from 0.08 to 4 s with envelope caps tied to it, 10 % octave or legato pairs, a fixed 3–4 s window from the onset;
- **labels:** values in the `ParamSpec` normalised domain (the ML boundary), one-hot categoricals, symmetric slots left to the loss, a loss mask from `observable()` (gauges, nuisances and dead parameters get no loss; no loss on releases the model's 2 s window cannot see), the semis against **the key the intake reads** (the told key), and ±50 ct of note noise drawn as `osc1.fine`, which decoding sets from the pitch offset (the model contract, F3s);
- **filters:** renders below −60 LUFS rejected; clipped renders kept; never a filter by duration;
- **splits by stream key** (`r3/train`, `r3/dev`, `r3/test`), never index ranges, so the sets are disjoint by construction; dedup by an exact hash of the masked canonical labels, then by pooled log-mel embeddings within about one just-noticeable step, re-run against every dev and test item after each refresh.

**The preset candidates are split into two disjoint halves:** anchors for `ds1`, and test targets for GR3, so no prior is fitted on a test preset. **Cost:** the CPU is the bottleneck (10–28 ms per dry 3 s render, 130–175 ms with the reverb, 65–110 ms per codec round trip); plan on 1–2 M renders consumed per run, a cache of about 250 k clean clips (48–72 GB) plus about 100 k pre-baked codec variants (about 19 GB), with 5–10 % of the shards re-rendered per epoch. `ds1` lives in `tools/datagen`, and features reach `ml/` through files it writes (resolved at F3s: the model contract below).

#### What lane DG built (`ds1` v1, 2026-10-22)

The design above, as built in `tools/datagen/` (`ds1.rs` and `ds1/`, `shards.rs`, `exclude.rs`; no new dependency: WAV, JSON lines and `synth.toml` are read by hand). Everything is deterministic and hermetic: no clock, network or random state reaches a sample.

**A sample is a pure function of `(ds1 version, stream key, run seed, index)`.** `sample_seed = derive(derive(derive(fnv1a64("ds1/1"), fnv1a64(stream)), run_seed), index)`, and each concern draws from its own keyed stream of that seed (`source`, `phrase`, `rate`, `noise`, `v1/musical`, `preset`, `uniform`), the way generator v1 keys its draws, so adding a concern never moves another's values. The stream key is part of the seed: `r3/train`, `r3/dev` and `r3/test` are disjoint by construction. A change to any draw is `ds1` v2 (it is in every seed, shard header and manifest).

| Decision | What `ds1` v1 does |
| --- | --- |
| v1's draws depend on the phrase (an octave pair reveals keytrack and velocity, a legato pair glide) | the `v1` source first draws the phrase **kind** it wants (90 % held, 5 % octave pair, 5 % legato pair), then takes the first of 64 attempts (40-bit seeds, `difficulty` 100, `amounts` 100 in every category, structure included, template 0, `musical` 100 / 50 / 0 at 60 / 25 / 15 %) whose generator phrase is that kind; the sampler then draws its own notes of that kind. An octave pair has two velocities and is detached; a legato pair has one velocity, overlaps and never spans an octave (it would reveal keytrack) |
| Phrase | key uniform over MIDI 24-84, velocity 30-127, hold log-uniform 0.08-4 s (pairs: 0.08-1.5 s), a 3.0-4.0 s window in quarters; a hold can outlast the window (the release is then not in the window and has no loss) |
| `preset` source (25 %) | an anchor chosen uniformly, every continuous value moved by a normal step of sigma 0.05-0.2 (one per sample) in the normalised domain, each categorical and structure switch flipped with 4 %, each route's depth moved by the same sigma in 5 % steps, removed with 3 %, and 2 % of the empty grid cells given a route of 10-60 % |
| `uniform` source (15 %) | every entry uniform over a **safe range** (semitones within two octaves, resonance at most 80 %, drive at most 12 dB, envelope times at most 1.5 s, the low-pass cutoff above the fundamental, ...), each structure switch 50 %, each route cell 12 % (25 % for velocity) with a depth of 5-100 % of either sign |
| Note noise | `osc1.fine`, the layout's context entry: 25 % in tune, the rest uniform over +-50 ct in tenths of a cent |
| Rates | 48 kHz 50 %, 44.1 kHz 25 %, 22.05 kHz 12.5 %, 16 kHz 12.5 %, rendered natively (the features are rate-invariant; this exercises the intake) |
| Render what you label | the patch is canonicalised under the sample's phrase ([`OutputLayout::canonicalise`]) before it is rendered, so what is heard is what is labelled; the label is `OutputLayout::encode(patch, &Hearing::sample(&Cue::from_intake(&intake), notes, length_ms))` on the clip the intake was given |
| Filters | a render at or below -60 LUFS (`quiet`) and anything the pipeline refuses is skipped and counted by code (`quiet`, `rekey`, `off_layout`, `features_silent`, `intake_*`, `excluded_*`, `near_gr3_*`, `duplicate_label`, `v1_phrase_match`, ...); clipped renders are kept; **no filter by duration** |
| Anchors | 11 byte copies of the anchor half (`ds1/anchors/*.patch.json`, taken at the freeze so that a retune of a candidate never moves v1's samples); a test compares them with `synth.toml`'s `anchor` list and the exclusion lists, and building the sampler against a list that names one is an error |
| Dedup | an exact SHA-256 of the masked canonical label (the mask, then the label with the unmasked values zeroed) within a shard; the near-duplicate test below is against GR3 and, in `exclude check --against`, against other runs. A pooled-embedding dedup across the whole run is not built: `check` reports the exact duplicates across shards (0 in the runs below) |

**Projection onto layout 1** (`ds1 anchors`; the pooled log-mel distance between the preset and its projection at the bank's reference note; one just-noticeable step is 0.125 nepers): the template with the preset's 33 continuous values, 7 categoricals, 4 structure switches and grid routes copied in. **The shard file** (format 1, `shards.rs`; frozen like the model file's: a change is format 2). Little-endian; the arrays are structure-of-arrays, so `numpy.fromfile` (or `array.array.frombytes`) reads each with one call, at the offsets the header gives:

```text
bytes 0..8     "ENTHSHRD"
bytes 8..12    u32 format = 1
bytes 12..16   u32 H, the length of the header JSON
bytes 16..16+H the header (UTF-8 JSON, schema "enth.ds1-shard/1"), padded with spaces up to byte 1024
byte 1024..    the arrays in the header's order, each starting at a multiple of 64
```

| Array | dtype | Shape | What |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `mel` | `<f4` | `[n, 3, 128, 64]` | feature set 1's log-mel (resolution, block of 1/64 s of the 2 s window, band) |
| `context` | `<f4` | `[n, 5]` | the told key minus 60, the pitch offset (semitones), pitched, the hold (s), the band limit (log2) |
| `label` | `<f4` | `[n, 102]` | layout 1's row: normalised continuous values, one-hot categoricals, structure and route presence as 0 or 1, route depth as `(depth / 100 + 1) / 2` |
| `mask` | `<f4` | `[n, 102]` | **1.0 where the entry carries a loss, else 0.0** (an `f32`, so a loss multiplies it without a cast; the "Who computes what" table below said `u8` before DG) |
| `index`, `seed`, `gen_seed` | `<u8` | `[n]` | the sample's index in its stream; its seed; the generator-v1 seed of a `v1` sample (else 0) |
| `rate`, `source` | `<u4`, `\|u1` | `[n]` | the clip's sample rate (Hz); 0 `v1`, 1 `preset`, 2 `uniform` |

The header also has `n`, the stream, the run seed, the shard number and the window `first_index`, `candidates` (the shard holds the `n <= candidates` samples that were not skipped, in index order). **`manifest.json`** (`enth.ds1-shards/1`, pretty JSON with sorted keys: the same bytes for the same run, and its SHA-256, which `gen` prints, is what TR's exporter copies) holds the `ds1` version, kind, stream, role, run seed, count, shard size, kept and skips by code and by source, the engine version, **the feature set** (id, name, inputs, context meaning), **the layout** (id, name, generator, template, width, SHA-256, the 102 entry names), **the loss scale**, the arrays, the sampler (mixture, rates, anchors with their hashes, the -60 LUFS), the exclusion lists' SHA-256 and what is asserted, the GR3 references (counts, which could not be labelled, which files are not written yet), warnings, and per shard its file, window, kept, bytes, SHA-256, skips and sources. `shards verify` re-checks every one.

**The loss scale** (`ds1/jnd.rs`): `jnd.scale[i]` is one just-noticeable step of entry `i` in the normalised domain, so TR never re-implements `ParamSpec`. The steps are the propose v0 table's (25 ct for pitch, 10 % for times, frequencies and ratios, 2 dB for levels, in dB or as a percentage) and, for the units that table does not name, generator v1's gameplay steps (`priors::jnd`: 10 points for a percentage, 1/6 octave, 30 degrees). A step in plain units is a different width in the normalised domain wherever the scale is not linear (a time on a power scale, a cutoff on a log scale), so `jnd.table` has, per continuous entry, the width at 17 evenly spaced normalised values (taken upward, downward at the top of the range) for linear interpolation, and `scale` takes the value at 0.5. A categorical, structure or route-presence entry is a whole step (1); a route depth is 5 % of its 200 % range (0.025).

**Exclusions** (`exclude.rs`): the three lists are parsed strictly (an entry type this build does not know is an error, so a newer list is never half-read) and hashed into every manifest. A sample is skipped, never kept, when:

| Entry | Asserted | Skip |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `seed-range` | its seed and, for a `v1` sample, its generator seed lie outside `770000000000..770999999999` (about 0.09 % of v1 draws fall in it: 1 of 1,000 candidates in the run below) | `excluded_seed` |
| `stream` | the run's stream is not listed, except the test stream, which is the one the lists reserve for `ds1` itself (`role` `test`) | the run refuses |
| `code`, `fingerprint` | a `v1` sample's code (long form: a `ds1` spec is no preset's, so it has no short one), the **spec** every listed code names, and its fingerprint | `excluded_code`, `excluded_fingerprint` |
| rule 3 | a `v1` sample is not within one minimum step of a listed GR3 generator target at any phrase (the bank's `near_duplicate`) | `near_gr3_generator` |
| near duplicates in label space | no sample's label is within one just-noticeable step (every entry both carry, same discrete part) of a GR3 generator target's or a test preset's, **each rendered and read by the same intake** (172 labelled of 179 references: 5 refuse a re-key, 2 are silent; the owner's 8 G1 sounds are not written yet) | `near_gr3_target` |
| `preset-stem`, `preset-name`, `file-sha256` | no anchor is a test preset or a byte copy of one | the sampler will not build |
| `path`, `audio-sha256`, `degrade-*` | the bridge: no clip is under a listed path or has listed bytes, and no degradation record names the held-out pool, a held-out profile, variant or seed, or a listed source | the run stops (a leak is an error, not a skip) |

`exclude check DIR` re-reads a finished run independently of the pass that made it: the lists' hash (a run made against other lists is refused), the stored seeds, a **re-draw of every sample from its key** (a sampler that moved is caught), the lists, rule 3 and the label test again, with `--against DIR` also the run's labels against another run's (train against dev and test). Tests prove each entry type: an item whose seed, code or fingerprint is listed, or whose label equals a reference's, does not enter a shard (`tests/ds1.rs`, `ds1/pipeline.rs`).

### Real audio is a hypothesis

The roadmap's "unlabelled real recordings train the audio side" is a hypothesis to test, not a plan: in 2026 papers real audio helped through render rewards (GRPO in DDSynth-RL, SynthRL) and barely as a passive input (about 617 k unpaired clips added almost nothing in Hayes 2026). The first use is **search-distilled pseudo-labels**: model + search on real one-shots, keep the good fits as labelled pairs, retrain, capped at about 30 % of the data and listened to batch by batch. A learned embedding proxy is close to the banned "differentiable copy of the engine", so it waits for your OK (default no).

### The model: propose v0 (lane TR)

| Part | Choice |
| --- | --- |
| Input | Feature set 1 (the model contract): the intake clip's first 2 s at −23 LUFS, `enth-analysis` log-mel (64 bands, 30 Hz–16 kHz) at three resolutions scaled to the file's own rate, on a time grid in seconds, plus a context of the told key, the pitch offset, the hold and the band limit (no f0 or loudness track: the pitch is in the context, the loudness in the mel); pitch-relative (the semis against the key the intake reads). An MFCC input is worth an ablation (Shier) |
| Encoder | A small 2D CNN, about 1–4 M parameters, run once; it also exports its embedding for retrieval |
| Heads | One logit per enum item (the arg-max decodes), one logit per enable flag and route presence; route presence and depth on a fixed source × destination grid (no slot symmetry); continuous values in the normalised domain (layout 1) |
| Candidates | **K = 8 hypothesis head sets, winner-takes-all** (annealed or relaxed) **plus a confidence head** that ranks them before rendering: K hypotheses in one forward pass |
| Loss | Masked by `observable()` and the model's window; the two LFOs by the min over the two permutations (`osc1` and `osc2` are not symmetric in layout 1); continuous error in JND units (25 ct; 10 % for times and Hz; 2 dB) with a Huber loss; cross-entropy for discrete heads |
| Pipeline | Render all K, rank, refine by successive halving with R3a's other starts; **the heuristic start is always a candidate**, and the cap includes inference |
| Must beat | Search alone at equal wall-clock (GR3), k-NN retrieval, and itself without search (a diagnostic) |

**Why not flows or diffusion first:** they are the best published for symmetric spaces, but take 20–100 network evaluations per sample with models of about 40 M parameters, and flow matching lost to autoregressive decoding on a discrete-heavy FM synth (DDSynth-RL). If WTA collapses or fails GR3 in-domain, move up: conditional flow matching or masked discrete diffusion, then render-reward fine-tuning, then partial noising of the input at inference.

#### The full run: stoppable, crash-safe, and an A/B (lane TR2, 2026-10-22; running)

**Pausable at any point.** `python -m enth_ml.train stop RUN_DIR` (or a `STOP` file, SIGTERM, SIGINT) ends the current step, writes a checkpoint and exits; `resume RUN_DIR` carries on from the newest valid checkpoint **at the exact sample** (the checkpoint holds the epoch's shard list and the samples consumed, the optimiser, scaler, WTA state, RNGs and counters; with no wall-clock term in the schedule the LR and temperature follow the step). Checkpoints every 25 minutes (newest two kept) plus `best.pt`, each written atomically with a SHA-256, so a crash costs at most one interval and a torn file falls back to the older one. CPU tests prove a stopped-and-resumed run and a `kill -9`-and-resumed run end with the same weights and logged losses as an uninterrupted one. The commands and the coordinator's one-line runbook: [ml/README.md](../ml/README.md), "Stop, resume and crash safety" and "The coordinator's runbook".

### Our own scalar kernels (lane ONX)

Inference runs in `enth-solver::propose` (L5, pure: no I/O, threads or clocks; wasm32), with the weights passed in as bytes:

| File | What |
| --- | --- |
| `pack.rs` | `ModelPack::parse(&[u8])`: the versioned header, sha256, fixture tests |
| `ops.rs` | Conv1d and Conv2d (stride, padding, dilation, groups), Linear, LayerNorm (BatchNorm folded at export), ReLU, GELU, SiLU, tanh, sigmoid, max, average and global pooling, softmax, residual add and concat, multi-head attention as Linear + matmul + softmax: a fixed summation order, no `mul_add`, transcendentals through the pinned `libm` |
| `net.rs` | `Net::run`: the layers listed in the pack |
| `features.rs` | The model's input from `enth-analysis`'s log-mel on the scalar FFT path |
| `decode.rs` | Outputs → patches: normalised → plain through `ParamSpec`, onto the template's free set |

- **Budgets:** at most about 4 M parameters (f16 in the file, at most 8 MB; f16 → f32 is exact), at most about 2 GFLOP per inference, the encoder run once. int8 only later, with integer kernels written to be bit-exact.
- **Measured** (report 03, a 0.8 M-parameter CNN, loaded machine): `tract` 62 ms, `rten` 18–29 ms, our naive scalar code 159 ms, all far under the budget; a blocked version with a fixed order would likely be 2–3× faster and stay bit-exact (an estimate). Bit for bit over 512 outputs: our kernels 512/512 on x86-64, `target-cpu=native` and wasm32; `tract` native against wasm32 37–48/512; `rten` changes with its thread count.
- **ONNX** stays the export and parity format: ONNX Runtime in `ml/`, and a dev-only `tools/model-check` (exempt) that compares our kernels with ONNX Runtime and `tract-nnef` within 1e-5 on exported fixtures and pins our exact output hashes on every CI platform.
- **Fallback** if the model outgrows the op set: `tract-nnef` only (never `tract-onnx`, which does not build for wasm32), behind an `infer-tract` feature. It needs your OK for `cc`, adds about 15 MB per plugin binary and gives up bit-exactness. **Rejected** for the product: `ort` (a C++ library), `candle`, and `rten` in the core (its own thread pool).

#### What lane ONX built (2026-10-22)

**The kernels** (`propose/ops.rs`): every op of op set 1 exactly as `pack.rs`'s table defines it, without the batch axis, in `f32`. Determinism, measured, not assumed:

- **A fixed order per output, plain `+` and `×`.** A convolution sums its group's input channels, then kernel rows, then kernel columns (padding skipped), then adds the bias; `Linear` sums `i` ascending, then the bias; `MatMul` sums `q` ascending; `LayerNorm` is two-pass (`μ`, then `Σ(x − μ)²`, ε inside the root); pools sum or compare row-major; softmax subtracts the maximum, sums in order and divides. Loops run several outputs side by side (the compiler vectorises across outputs, never within a sum), so the naive code stays fast and the bits stay fixed. `expf`, `erff` and `tanhf` come from the pinned `libm`; comparisons are written out (never `f32::max`, whose `max(−0, +0)` is unspecified).
- **Every result is flushed in code**: after every product, partial sum and transcendental, a value at or below `f32::MIN_POSITIVE` in magnitude becomes `+0`. So no operation reads a subnormal (DAZ has nothing to change) and none keeps one (FTZ has nothing to change), whether the CPU detects tininess after rounding (x86) or before (Arm): flushing the smallest normal too covers both. The contract's "weights and inputs are never subnormal" was not enough: a product of two normals, or a sum of two, can be subnormal (an `underflow` fixture does it on purpose, about half of 24,576 products). **Negative control:** with the flush removed, that fixture's hash differs under FTZ.
- **`Net::build`** (`propose/net.rs`) infers every shape op by op, refuses a graph no exporter makes (`model_malformed`, naming the node: channels against groups, a kernel past the padded input, a broadcast, a `Reshape`'s count, a permutation's rank, a slice past its axis, a stride, pad or dilation above 2¹⁶) and declared outputs that disagree with the graph, recounts the FLOPs from the shapes (refused over 2³¹, `model_too_large`), and bounds memory before anything is allocated: one value at most 2²⁴ `f32`s, the values alive at once at most 2²⁵ along the node order, each freed after its last reader (`model_too_large`, a new `Budget::Memory`; a chain of `Concat`s, which costs 0 FLOPs, is caught there). `Net::run(&ModelInput)` flushes its input and runs the nodes; `Outputs::sha256` is the output hash a golden pins.
- **Additive in `pack.rs`:** `Budget::Memory`; `f32_to_f16` (round to nearest even); `Val` and `ModelBuilder`, a format-1 writer that reproduces the pinned format-1 fixture byte for byte (fixtures, goldens and tools build models with it).

**The output hashes** (`tests/propose_kernels.rs`): six hand-built models covering every op (the K = 8 heads; a 2-D conv stack with stride, padding, dilation, groups, both pools and both global pools; a 1-D conv stack with GELU; a dense mix of LayerNorm, both `Linear` forms, the activations, broadcasting, both softmax axes, `Transpose`, `MatMul` of two activations, `Concat` and a strided `Slice`; a CNN with a four-head attention block, 11 MFLOP; the underflow model), each with its output SHA-256 and FLOP recount pinned, and the same hash under FTZ off, FTZ only and FTZ+DAZ (x86-64) or FZ off and on (aarch64). **Measured locally, all identical:** dev and release, `target-cpu=native`, `+fma,+avx2`, and wasm32-wasip1 under wasmtime with and without simd128.

**The CI step** (`golden.yml`, every leg: Linux x86-64 and aarch64, Windows MSVC, macOS arm64, wasm32 under wasmtime with and without simd128; the native-flag pair on Linux x86-64): `propose_kernels`, `propose_parity`, `propose_ftz` and `--lib propose::` (feature set 1's pins with the kernels' unit tests), release. The Linux x86-64 and wasm32 commands ran locally; the cross-target clippy passes for Windows and macOS. **The aarch64, MSVC and macOS arm64 legs are proven by CI after the merge** (phase-3's open check "our kernels bit-exact on macOS arm64 and MSVC").

**Parity with ONNX Runtime** (`tests/propose_parity.rs`, within 1e-5): four ONNX graphs built with `onnx.helper` by `ml/enth_ml/export/parity.py` (2-D convs and pools with a folded `BatchNormalization`; 1-D convs and pools; a dense mix with `Mul(x, Sigmoid(x))` as SiLU, two softmax axes, `Flatten`; a CNN with a four-head attention block and `f16` matrices), run through ONNX Runtime 1.31.0 (one thread, graph optimisation off) on a synthetic input, converted by `ml/enth_ml/export/onnx_import.py`, and committed under `tests/fixtures/propose/` (about 55 KB; each model names its input seed, so no input file). ### The model file

- A separate, versioned file, `enth-model-<id>.bin`, beside the plugin; embedded only if it stays at 2 MB or less (bundling copies the weights into both the CLAP and the VST3 binary). The header (the model contract below): format, op set, feature set, layout, generator and template ids, the model id, the layout's SHA-256 (which covers the parameter addresses, their normalisation and the label rules), the engine version, the `ds1` version and the training manifest's SHA-256, sha256.
- The plugin looks next to its binary, then in `<Documents>/enth/Models/`; the CLI takes `--model`. A missing file, or a header that does not match this build's catalogue and template, means "Create from sound" runs R3a and says so; a newer format is refused like `code_too_new`.
- No network code: an update is a plain file download, like a content pack. The R2 pack format may carry models later.

#### The input: feature set 1 (`propose-logmel/1`)

Computed by `ModelInput::compute(clip, sample_rate, &Cue)` (or `ModelInput::from_intake`), **in Rust, for training and for inference alike**: `tools/datagen` calls it on every training clip, a create job on the upload. Pure, and the same bits on every platform and under any FTZ/DAZ setting (the scalar FFT path, the in-house loudness, `libm`, sums in `f64` in a fixed order; `tests/propose_ftz.rs`). `feature_set_1_is_pinned` holds the hashes of six inputs (44.1 kHz, a 4 kHz band limit, 16 kHz, a 0.1 s clip, unpitched noise, 96 kHz), so a change to anything it reads fails until it ships as feature set 2.

| Step | Rule | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The window | **The first 2 s of the intake's clip** (`Intake::audio`, the file's own rate, the onset at `perf.on_s` about 5–9 ms in), from its first sample; zero-padded when shorter | The start of create's scored window (`SCORE_S`, 3 s): the model sees what the search scores. Short sounds are padded, never refused |
| Level | The window's audio to −23 LUFS with the **in-house BS.1770 meter** (`enth_challenge::loudness`), as `sound/1` does; at or below −70 LUFS: `features_silent` | `ebur128` uses `std` maths, so its gain differs in the last bits between platforms, and every log-mel value with it |
| Log-mel | `FeatureExtractor::extract_unnormalised`, scalar FFT: 64 mel bands, 30 Hz–16 kHz **in Hz**, at three resolutions **scaled to the rate** (`FeatureCfg::rate_scaled_fft`): `n = 2 · round(N · sr / 96,000)` for N = 2048, 1024, 512 (exactly those at 48 kHz; 1882, 940, 470 at 44.1 kHz; 4096, 2048, 1024 at 96 kHz), hop `n / 2` | Bins stay about 23.4 Hz wide and windows the same length in seconds, so a band's level does not follow the rate: with fixed sizes a noise band read 0.54 nepers louder at 16 kHz and 0.35 quieter at 96 kHz than at 48 (the judge's probe); now at most 0.013 from 16 to 96 kHz (`band_levels_do_not_follow_the_rate`). The option is off for every other analysis, so `sound/1` and every golden are unchanged |
| **The time grid, in seconds** | Each resolution's frames read as a piecewise-constant track (half a hop either side of each frame's centre), averaged over **128 blocks of 1/64 s** weighted by overlap (mel magnitudes, then the natural log) | Frames per second follow the rounding of the sizes; on the grid, one pluck's onset lands in the same block (±1) at 48, 44.1, 32, 22.05 and 16 kHz (tested) |
| Empty bands | A band whose upper edge is above `min(sr / 2, bandwidth_hz)` is set to `logf(1e-5)`, bit for bit the analysis's floor of a silent band (so padded silence and a masked band hold one value) | Determinism point 7: a low-rate or band-limited file has empty top bands, never resampled; the same mask as OBJ's distance and the k-NN embedding |

`context`, from **the intake's** inferred performance and bandwidth (`Cue::from_intake`; never a `--perf` override, which changes how a job renders, not what the model is told):

| # | Value |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | the MIDI key the intake reads − 60 (semitones): **the told key** |
| 1 | `12 · log2(f0 / f(key))` when the intake found a pitch, else 0 (semitones, about ±0.5; `Cue::pitch_offset`) |
| 2 | 1 when pitched, else 0 |
| 3 | the hold, capped at 4 s (`ds1`'s holds are 0.08–4 s) |
| 4 | `log2(min(sr / 2, bandwidth_hz, 16 kHz) / 16 kHz)` (octaves, at most 0) |

So the model is pitch-relative, as `ds1`'s labels are: it predicts `osc1.semi` and `osc2.semi` against the told key, and `osc1.fine` comes from `context[1]`. No value of `mel` or `context` is subnormal. No f0 or loudness track (report 01's "plus f0 and loudness tracks"): the loudness is the mel's sum, the pitch is in the context, and a pitch track is a feature set 2 if dev shows vibrato or glide proposals need it. The context carries no SNR or clipping reading (augmentation teaches robustness; adding them later is feature set 2). `ModelInput::sha256` is the input's hash a result records.

#### The output: layout 1 (`propose-layout/1`)

**The label space is generator v1's draw set on template 0** (`priors::v1`'s table over the frozen starter superset), because `ds1` reuses v1's draws: the 40 parameters v1 draws, the four modules it may switch on, and its route sources and destinations as a grid. One hypothesis is one row of 102 `f32`s:

| Segment | Offsets | Entries | Each value | Decoded as |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Continuous | 0–32 | 33 `Float` parameters (`voice.glide`; `osc1` semi, pw, position; `osc2` semi, fine, level, pw, position; `noise1.level`; `filter1` cutoff, resonance, keytrack, drive; `comp1` threshold, ratio, attack, release; `reverb1` wet, room, decay, high cut; `env1` attack, decay, sustain, release, velocity; `env2` attack, decay, sustain, release; both LFO rates) | the `ParamSpec` normalised value, 0–1 | clamped to 0–1, then `ParamSpec::from_norm` |
| Categorical | 33–65 | 7 groups, one logit per item: `voice.mode` 3, `osc1.wave` 5, `osc2.wave` 5, `noise1.color` 2, `filter1.mode` 4, `lfo1.shape` 7, `lfo2.shape` 7 | logits | the arg-max (ties: the lower item) |
| Structure | 66–69 | `osc2`, `noise1`, `comp1`, `reverb1` (the modules the template has off) | logits | on when above 0 |
| Route presence | 70–85 | 4 sources (`env2`, `lfo1`, `lfo2`, `velocity`) × 4 destinations (`osc1.fine`, `osc1.level`, `osc1.pw`, `filter1.cutoff`), source-major | logits | a route when above 0 and its depth is not 0 |
| Route depth | 86–101 | the same cells | `(depth / 100 + 1) / 2` | `(2n − 1) · 100` % of the destination's range |

Besides the row, layout 1 has one **context entry**: `osc1.fine` is never predicted; decoding sets it to `100 · context[1]` cents, within ±50, when the clip is pitched (else the template's 0), so a proposal starts in tune, and `ds1` draws its ±50 ct note noise as `osc1.fine`.

- **The order is built from the template and the catalogue** (`OutputLayout::build`): `voice` first, then the template's modules in file order (generators, voice effects, effects, modulators), each in spec order; route sources are the template's modules in that order, then the built-in sources.
- **A `Hearing`** says how a sound is heard: the told key, the pitch offset and the phrase, built at run time (enth-challenge's `PhraseView` and `observable_view`: `ds1` samples millions of phrases, which cannot be `'static`). `Hearing::sample(&cue, notes, length_ms)` is a `ds1` sample's (DG); `Hearing::job(&cue, &target.perf)` is a create job's (MS): one note on the job's key, its hold, its scored window. Its errors are `hearing_phrase`, `hearing_key` and `hearing_times`.
- **Labels** (`OutputLayout::encode(patch, &hearing)`, for DG) canonicalise under **the sample's phrase**: a route the phrase cannot reveal is removed, then every layout parameter it cannot reveal goes back to the template's value. Then **the semis are re-keyed to the told key**: `osc1.semi` and `osc2.semi` (where heard) move by the phrase's first key − the told key, so two sounds the model cannot tell apart get one label (the judge's pair: a semi of +12 at C3 and of 0 at C4, both read as C4, had one input and two labels; 16 of 24 v1 targets heard at C3 re-key). A re-key that leaves ±48 semitones, or meets a heard keytrack (which follows the played key, not the semis; only pairs reveal it), is refused (`rekey`). The loss mask has no loss on a parameter the phrase does not reveal, on the depth of an absent route, and, **when the first note-off falls at or after the model's 2 s window** (the intake's `on_s` plus the phrase's hold), on the envelopes' releases, which the model cannot see (up to about 18 % of `ds1`'s samples). `encode` refuses a patch that is not on the layout (`off_layout`: other modules, a heard parameter outside the layout away from the template's value, `osc1.fine` beyond ±50 cents, a route off the grid), so **every `ds1` sample is a template-0 patch that differs only in layout entries and `osc1.fine`**: DG projects the preset anchors onto the layout before rendering (`osc1.sync`, reverb `early`, macro routes and the like go back to the template's values), and reports how far that moves them.
- **Render what you label.** Canonicalising resets what the phrase cannot reveal, which is not always silent (a keytrack under one note is a cutoff move; a velocity amount under one velocity scales a depth), so `ds1` renders the patch `OutputLayout::canonicalise` leaves under the sample's phrase (generator v1 already draws only what the phrase reveals; projected presets need it).
- **Decoding** (`OutputLayout::decode(row, &hearing)`, for MS) writes a hypothesis onto the template (structure, categoricals, values, routes), canonicalises it under **the job's performance**, re-keys the semis from the told key to the job's key (they differ only under a `--perf` override; past ±48 the hypothesis is dropped, `hypothesis_rekey`), and sets `osc1.fine` from the pitch offset. A row with a NaN or an infinity is dropped (`hypothesis_non_finite`), never guessed; a subnormal value reads as 0. So **a label is exactly what decoding gives back when the job plays the sample's phrase** (structure, categoricals and routes exact; values within the `f32` rounding of the normalised domain; tested under the held phrase, a 2.8 s pad and a 0.3 s note, and on 80 v1 targets). Under the held phrase the pad's 2 s attack would decode to 5 ms (the judge's measurement): that is why decoding follows the job.
- **Under one note, 14 of the 102 values never reach a decoded patch** (`voice.mode`, `voice.glide`, `filter1.keytrack`, `env1.velocity` and the velocity row of the grid, measured by the judge). They stay in the layout for phrases that reveal them; TR judges dev metrics on decoded patches.
- **Symmetric slots** are the loss's job (TR): the two LFOs with their route rows can swap, so TR takes the minimum over the two permutations (continuous 31↔32, categorical 52–58↔59–65, presence 74–77↔78–81, depth 90–93↔94–97, masks swapped too). `osc1` and `osc2` are not symmetric here (sync and every oscillator route target `osc1`, and `osc1` is always on).
- **The outputs** are `hypotheses` `[Kₘ, 102]` and `confidence` `[Kₘ]` (logits), Kₘ = 8 for propose v0. More outputs (an embedding for retrieval) may be added. The graph outputs exactly what decoding reads: continuous values in 0–1 (a sigmoid in the graph, or raw values that decoding clamps), logits elsewhere; any input standardisation is `Mul` and `Add` constants in the graph, never Python preprocessing.

**How the Kₘ hypotheses join a create job** (lane MS; `create::Inputs::proposals`, unchanged since F1s): each row that decodes becomes a `Start` with `Provenance::Model { model: <model id>, hypothesis: k, confidence: Some(logit) }`, in **descending confidence** (ties: the lower k), proposed after the k-NN neighbours. Round 0 screens every start once (the heuristic's, the templates', up to 16 neighbours', and the Kₘ proposals: about 28 renders), the heuristic start is always kept, and the best K − 1 of the rest become branches, so a hypothesis competes on its rendered distance, and the confidence only orders the proposals and labels them. The result records every start as a patch, so a replay needs neither the bank nor the model file; MS adds the model's id and file SHA-256 and `ModelInput::sha256` to `enth.create/1`'s settings, additively. **The free set still uses the held phrase** (`create::free_set`): MS aligns it with the job's performance (`observable_view` under the job's `Hearing`), or a decoded long attack is never searched.

#### The layout hash (the catalogue hash)

`OutputLayout::canonical_text` has one line per entry with **exactly what decoding and labelling read**: the address, its spec's kind, range, scale, unit and items, **the spec's `from_norm` at 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 and 1** (so a change to a normalisation formula refuses old models instead of decoding them wrongly), the template's value and the offsets, and for each route cell the destination's spec; then **the rules**: how each segment decodes, the context entry, the re-keyed semis and the keytrack, the 2 s window's releases, and which phrase each side canonicalises under. A model's header carries its SHA-256; the reader compares it with this build's.

| A change to the catalogue or the rules | Layout-1 models |
| --- | --- |
| A new parameter, enum item, module kind or route source anywhere else; a migration that adds a key with a neutral default | **still load** (the text does not move) |
| A field a layout entry reads: a range, scale, unit, item list, normalisation, the template's value of a layout or context entry | refused (`model_mismatch`); ship layout 2 or freeze the old values |
| A label or decoding rule (the re-key, the window, the context entry, a segment's decoding) | refused (`model_mismatch`): a new layout |
| A renamed key or a removed module the layout lists | refused (`model_mismatch`, the layout no longer builds) |
| Generator v2, another template | a new layout id: layout 1 stays defined on generator 1 and template 0 |

`layout-v1.txt` pins the text (`the_v1_layout_is_pinned`, after `random/catalogue-v1.txt`'s pattern); its SHA-256 is ``2463ccea7ea768f368a80a7634369b93b00891f148560ad84989a15c8e3224a8``. The fields generator v1 reads are already pinned there, so the two tests trip together: the hash adds no constraint beyond generator v1's pin (the judge's ruling).

#### The model file (`ENTHMODL`, format 1)

`enth-model-<id>.bin`, little-endian, read by `ModelPack::parse(&[u8])`; `pack.rs` has the byte table:

| Part | Fields |
| --- | --- |
| Header, 256 bytes | magic `ENTHMODL`; format 1; header length 256; op set; feature set; layout; generator; template (a generator template's id: templates are frozen per id, so the id is its version, and the layout hash covers the values decoding reads from it); Kₘ; H; the model id (32 bytes, `[a-z0-9][a-z0-9._-]*`); the engine that rendered the training data (16 bytes, information only); **the layout SHA-256**; parameters; FLOPs (information only); the four table lengths; the data section's offset (a multiple of 64) and length; **the `ds1` version** (`u16`, information only); the info table's length; **the training manifest's SHA-256** (information only); 24 reserved zero bytes; **the file's SHA-256**, taken over the whole file with its own 32 bytes read as zeros |
| Inputs | name, dtype, dims: the feature set's, in its order |
| Tensors | name, dtype (1 `f32`, 2 `f16`), dims, offset into the data section (a multiple of 64, so a reader may map the weights in place); stored in table order without overlap; each at most 2²² elements; every value finite and no `f32` value subnormal |
| Nodes | op, inputs, outputs, attributes (`i32`; a float is its bit pattern; checked where no shape is needed), then the input value ids. Value ids run inputs, tensors, then node outputs in order; a node reads only earlier ids |
| Outputs | name, value id, dims (at most 2²⁰ elements): `hypotheses` `[Kₘ, H]` and `confidence` `[Kₘ]` at least |
| Info | key and value pairs (printable ASCII, a value up to 1,024 bytes), information only and covered by the SHA-256; a reader ignores the keys it does not know, so metadata (a training commit, data licences) never needs format 2 |
| Data | the tensors' bytes; the file ends where the header says |

**Op set 1** (the kernels ONX writes), in `f32`, **without the batch axis** (shapes `[C, L]` or `[C, H, W]`; the exporter remaps every axis; axes are never negative), explicit padding only, floor mode only: `Conv1d`, `Conv2d`, `Linear`, `MatMul`, `LayerNorm`, `Relu`, `Gelu`, `Silu`, `Tanh`, `Sigmoid`, `MaxPool1d`, `AvgPool1d`, `MaxPool2d`, `AvgPool2d`, `GlobalAvgPool`, `GlobalMaxPool`, `Softmax`, `Add`, `Mul`, `Concat`, `Reshape`, `Transpose`, `Sub` and `Slice` (the last two added at F3S: a class token and a difference, the likeliest op set 2 requests). **`pack.rs`'s table is normative**: each op's shapes, formula and attributes. "The ONNX operator of the same name" was not exact (the judge's S1), so the table pins:

| Op | Pinned |
| --- | --- |
| `LayerNorm` | over the last axis; biased variance; ε inside the root: `(x − μ) / √(σ² + ε) · γ + β` |
| `Gelu` | the erf form, `0.5 · x · (1 + erf(x / √2))` (ONNX opset 20, not PyTorch's `tanh` form) |
| `Silu`, `Linear`, the convolutions, the global pools | `x · sigmoid(x)` (no ONNX op); `x · wᵀ + b` (ONNX `Gemm` or `MatMul` + `Add`); ONNX `Conv`; ONNX `GlobalAveragePool` and `GlobalMaxPool`, which keep the reduced axes as 1s |
| `AvgPool` | padding **not counted** (ONNX `count_include_pad = 0`; PyTorch's default counts it); each pad below the kernel |
| `Reshape` | 1–8 dims, each at least 1 or one −1; **0 is refused** (ONNX would copy a dim) |
| `MatMul`, `Add`, `Mul`, `Sub` | both ranks at least 2 for `MatMul`, its leading axes broadcast; numpy broadcasting for the element-wise ops |
| `Softmax` | the maximum subtracted first |

`BatchNorm` is folded at export; multi-head attention is `Linear` + `Reshape` + `Transpose` + `MatMul` + `Softmax`; pooling over one axis is `Transpose` + `GlobalAvgPool` (or `AvgPool2d` with a full-length kernel); squeeze-excitation is `Mul` with broadcasting. **`f16` weights widen to `f32` exactly** (every binary16 value is a binary32 value; `f16_to_f32` is tested on its edge cases and for order over every positive finite value), so storage costs no bit-exactness; TR's parity runs against the `f16`-rounded weights, and keeps a tensor in `f32` where rounding costs accuracy. A kernel's summation order is ONX's and identical on every platform; a later build may change it, which moves a model's output golden but never a replay.

#### Refusals

`ModelPack::parse` checks, in order: the magic, the format (a newer one stops there: a newer format may lay out the rest differently), the file's size, the length the header gives, the SHA-256, the reserved fields, the versions, the id, Kₘ and the parameter budget, then the tables: **every count against the bytes its entries need at least, before anything is allocated**, every product of dims checked, each node's attributes, the info table, the data and every weight; then **this build**: the layout's hash and width, the inputs, the outputs. **Anyone can compute a SHA-256, so a valid hash does not mean a sane file**: whatever the bytes, `parse` refuses and never panics or aborts (a file with nodes = `u32::MAX` and a valid hash used to abort the process; a property test now feeds it mutated, rehashed files).

| Code | When | Create says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `not_a_model` | not the magic, or under 256 bytes | runs without a model |
| `model_too_new` | a newer format, op set, feature set, layout, generator or template (as `code_too_new` for a newer code version or generator) | "update enth to use it" |
| `model_truncated`, `model_hash`, `model_malformed` | shorter than its header, damaged, or a field no writer makes (a count no file can hold, an attribute out of range, a misaligned or subnormal tensor, a layout named with another generator or template) | "download it again" |
| `model_too_large` | over a budget | runs without it |
| `model_mismatch` | well formed, but the layout hash, width, inputs or outputs are not this build's | "use the model released with this build" |

**Every refusal means "Create from sound" runs R3a and says why** (the code, the message and the hint); a missing file is the shell's, and the same. Feature errors (`features_silent`, …), a hearing error and a hypothesis that does not decode drop the model's proposals or that one hypothesis, and say so.

#### Budgets

At most **2²² parameters** (about 4 M, checked at load, each tensor and the sum) and **2³¹ FLOPs per inference** (about 2 G), counted as `2 ·` the multiply-adds of `Conv1d`, `Conv2d`, `Linear` and `MatMul`, plus 1 per output element of every other op (`Reshape`, `Transpose`, `Concat` and `Slice` count 0). **The header's FLOP figure is information only**: ONX's net recounts from the shapes at load and refuses a graph over the budget (`model_too_large`). A file of at most 17 MiB (every parameter in `f32`). Shipped models store weights in `f16`: about 8 MB at the cap, and embedded in the plugin only at 2 MB or less (the delivery rule above). The target is the front end's 0.2 s and inference's 1 s; TR aims at about 0.5 GFLOP.

#### Who computes what (DG's open check, resolved: files)

Phase-3's open check asked whether features reach `ml/` through `enth-py` (PyO3, L8) or through files `tools/datagen` writes. **Files**, for R3b v0:

1. `enth-datagen ds1` (DG, Rust) samples each patch (a template-0 patch on layout 1, presets projected) and its phrase, and renders **the patch canonicalised under its phrase** (render what you label);
2. DEG's chain and AUG (Python, ffmpeg as a subprocess) write degraded copies as files, which is how that chain already works (one ffmpeg run per stage, offline);
3. `enth-datagen features` (DG, Rust; as built `shards features`, with `ds1 render` writing the clean clips and a `samples.jsonl` for the chain to copy: see "What lane DG built") reads each clip, runs **the product's intake** (`Intake::new`), computes `ModelInput::from_intake`, and the label with `OutputLayout::encode(patch, &Hearing::sample(&Cue::from_intake(&intake), notes, length_ms))`, and writes shards; it skips and counts silent clips (`features_silent`), off-layout samples (`off_layout`) and refused re-keys (`rekey`), by code;
4. TR reads the shards: per sample `mel` `[3, 128, 64]` `f32`, `context` `[5]` `f32`, `label` `[102]` `f32`, `mask` `[102]` `f32` (0.0 or 1.0; DG's decision, so the loss multiplies it without a cast), and the shard manifest's feature id, layout id and layout SHA-256, generator, template, engine version and `ds1` version, plus a per-entry JND scale in the normalised domain computed in Rust (TR does not re-implement `ParamSpec`). TR's exporter copies the ids, the layout SHA-256, the `ds1` version and the run manifest's SHA-256 into the model's header.

Why files: the degradation chain runs offline through ffmpeg anyway, so nothing needs Rust inside the data loader; running the same intake as the product puts its misses (a late onset under noise, a missed codec cut, an octave reading) into training as they will happen at inference; shards are hashable and cacheable for the run manifest; and `enth-py` would add a new L8 crate, pyo3, maturin and a lock change for no gain yet. It stays the path if online augmentation in the loader is ever wanted. Shards keep `f32` (storing `f16` would train on rounded features the model never sees at inference; if DG must, it says so in the manifest). Python never computes a feature or a label. DG decodes codec outputs with the product's decoders where it can, and renders part of `ds1` at native rates other than 48 kHz (feature set 1 is rate-invariant, so this is for the intake, not the features).

### Training

PyTorch with uv in `ml/`, never shipped and in no cargo or CI build; features computed by `enth-analysis` (through files `tools/datagen` writes, the model contract), never re-implemented in Python. On the laptop's GPU overnight with FP16 autocast, under a memory cap, never beside heavy builds, and attended the first time; a rented GPU only if a run would exceed about 24 h, and only on your account (a first model plus ablations is about 20–40 GPU-hours). Every run has a manifest (commit, engine version, `ds1` version and stream keys, phrase and augmentation configs, per-sample seeds `derive(run_seed, id, epoch)`, `ffmpeg -version`, cache and `uv.lock` hashes) and a 1,000-clip augmented golden that catches tooling drift. Logs stay local. The artefact is the model file plus a 20-input output golden.

## The determinism contract

[design.md §5](design.md#5-determinism) has the rule; here it is in full.

1. **Inference is bit-exact on every platform and under any FTZ/DAZ setting.** Our kernels use plain `+` and `×` in a fixed order, no `mul_add`, and the pinned `libm`; no weight or input is subnormal, a result that can underflow is flushed in code, and decoding reads a subnormal value as 0; each model file has exact output hashes, checked on Linux, Windows, macOS arm64 and wasm32 with FTZ off, FTZ only and FTZ+DAZ. The features' pins (`feature_set_1_is_pinned`) run on the same legs: `rustfft`'s inner twiddles come from `std`'s `f64` `cos` and `sin`, as in every analysis.
2. **Search is deterministic given its starts**, its clip and its settings, for any worker count: merged batches are split back by index, and successive halving's schedule is a function of each branch's evaluation counts, never of time or of the budget.
3. **The budget and the wall-clock cap only truncate.** Successive halving's rungs sit at milestones in each branch's own renders (`first_rung · eta^r`), never at fractions of the budget, and the budget is a hard cap and nothing else, so a run capped by either is an **exact prefix** of a longer one (the coordinator's decision at F1s, after the judge measured a run capped after 12 rounds that a 342-render budget did not reproduce). A result records its settings and the renders it used, and the same job with `budget` = those renders (the **render-equivalent twin** GR3 uses; `CreateRecord::replay_cfg`) reproduces it on any machine; one 300 s run contains the 60 s run, and gives the whole anytime curve.
4. **Every result records its K starts as patches**, the input features' hash and the decoded audio's hash (`enth.create/1`, and the plugin's candidate list). The recorded starts make a replay independent of retrieval and inference (no bank or model file needed); they do not make it independent of decoding: **a replay needs the same clip bit for bit**, which the recorded hash checks. WAV and FLAC decode exactly; whether `nanomp3` and `lewton` do across CPUs is an open check.
5. **Snapping proposals** to a grid (report 04's R8) is an optional guard; with bit-exact kernels it is not needed, and with a drifting runtime it would not suffice (about three quarters of runs would still cross a rounding boundary).
6. **Training is not bit-reproducible** (GPU kernels); the artefact that must be reproducible is the hashed model file and its output golden.
7. **No resampler in the product path:** render and analysis run at the file's own rate, and the model's features put their bands in Hz, so a low-rate file just has empty top bands.

## GR3 and its hygiene

**The gate** ([roadmap.md](roadmap.md#r3--sound--patch-610-weeks)): on held-out sounds from our own synth, model + search beats search alone at equal time; on real samples, including held-out degraded uploads, a blind listening test prefers it; every result arrives in under 5 minutes. Report 04 §3 makes each clause testable; EVK and EVb run it, and `docs/gr3.md` (planned) will hold the protocol's details and the runs.

**Clause 1: held-out synth sounds, equal time.**

- **Arms** share the intake, the start set, the free set, the workers and the seeds: **A**, R3a's best configuration tuned on dev (the baseline must be the best search, not GR1's 1,500-render floor); **B**, model + search from the same budget; **C**, the model's proposals unrefined (a diagnostic); **D**, A given the true template (separates structure error from parameter error).
- **Equal time:** B's clock includes intake, features and inference. Timed runs hold `flock -x` with no lane running, at the plugin's worker count (4) and the CLI's (8), and log their renders: a timed run's **render-equivalent twin** is the same job with its renders as the budget, which reproduces it exactly on any machine (rungs never depend on the budget, so a capped run is a prefix of a longer one, and one 300 s run holds the 60 s run).
- **Endpoints, fixed before the first test run:** at 60 s, the pass rate (`sound/1` ≥ 90 against the target patch) by McNemar on discordant targets, and the per-target difference in JND units (Wilcoxon, Hodges–Lehmann interval). Secondary: anytime curves (median at 10, 30, 60 and 300 s), time to pass (Kaplan–Meier, censored at 300 s), `structure/1`, results per level and per degradation. **"Beats"** means better at 60 s (one-sided p < 0.05) and not worse at 300 s.
- **Goodhart guard:** both arms minimise a log-mel distance and `sound/1` is log-mel, so one metric no arm optimises is reported too (MFCC, or a CLAP cosine on final candidates).
- **Size and cost:** a sign test needs 69 targets to detect a 65 % win rate (37 for 70 %, 158 for 60 %), so about 120. 120 targets × 2 arms × 60 s is about 4 h of the machine alone, plus about 7 h for a 40-target 300 s tail: overnight, two or three full runs at most.

**Clause 2: blind listening on real and degraded sounds.**

- **60 references** in `content/gr3/` (the manifest is committed; audio only when it is CC0 or yours): 10 one-shots from other subtractive synths, 10 FM, wavetable or digital, 10 acoustic and percussive (short sounds under 200 ms included, never filtered by duration), 10 of your picks, and **20 degraded copies, judged against the clean original**.
- **The held-out chain** (seeded, its parameters in the manifest, impulse responses, noises and codec settings disjoint from training's): MP3 at 32–96 kbps, noise at 5–20 dB SNR plus hum, reverb RT60 0.4–2.5 s, clipping and limiting at +6 to +18 dB, phone-speaker EQ, stereo to mono; plus about 30 phone re-recordings of yours if you make them (the default is synthetic phone EQ).
- **The trial** (`enth-listen gr3`): the reference, then A, then B; "which is closer to the reference?", forced choice. Each arm's **top-ranked** candidate by its own objective, never a human pick. Every clip, the reference included, at −23 LUFS (the in-house BS.1770), a trial with a clip over −1 dBTP lowered as a whole, the A/B order counterbalanced. Six catch trials (two identical pairs, four against the Init patch); a listener under 80 % on attention is excluded. Friends get `--export-kit`: random-named, loudness-matched WAVs and an answers file for any OS and player.
- **The decision rule, fixed in advance:** B is preferred when the 95 % cluster-bootstrap lower bound (resampling references, then listeners) is above 50 % and the degraded subset's point estimate is at least 50 %. Strata are reported, not gated.
- **Size:** you hear 60 references plus 6 catch trials (20–25 min, resumable); two friends hear 30 each (about 15 min). Owner-only detects about a 70 % preference. A MUSHRA-style rating on 10 references with all four arms is optional and on the cut list.

**Clause 3: under 5 minutes.** A cap guarantees it, so the gate is on what the cap hides: the maximum time from file to last candidate over every test run, with the test host playing a 16-voice patch; **time to the first candidate at most 15 s**; and `process-stats` p99 and p99.9 with a search running (`--gate g1`), so a search never costs the DAW an xrun.

## Risks

Report 04 §4 has fourteen, each with its cheapest retirement. The ones that shape the plan:

| Risk | Retirement |
| --- | --- |
| Search fits the degradation and undoes the model | FE and OBJ before R3a ships; arm C; clean references |
| The domain gap: synth-trained proposals miss real sounds | R3a measures the real baseline first; a 10-sound pilot listen after the first small model; per-stratum reports |
| `sound/1` has no JND scale for a recording (it needs the target *patch*) | Raw distance and components for real audio; listening decides; an independent metric |
| Symmetric solutions make a regressor average modes | K winner-takes-all hypotheses; canonical slots; `structure/1` and best-of-K |
| Leakage into the held-out sets | The wave-1 freeze; disjoint preset halves; Freesound-id dedupe; disjoint impulse-response and noise pools |
| Real time and lifetime in the DAW | The separate cancellable pool; capped workers; `process-stats` under search |
| A wrong inferred performance (note, hold, chords) | The intake infers and warns; `--perf` overrides |
| Training compute on a laptop with tight RAM | Small models; overnight runs never beside builds; attended first; renting only with your OK |
