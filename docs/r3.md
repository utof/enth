# Sound → patch (R3): create from sound, then a model's proposals

R3 turns a sound file into editable patches, fully locally: "Create from sound" in the CLI and the plugin. It ships in two steps. **R3a** has no ML: a robust intake, a degradation-aware objective, a portfolio of starts with k-NN retrieval, and K branches refined by search on a cancellable pool. **R3b** adds a small model whose K hypotheses become extra starts, run on our own scalar kernels. This page is the design of record; the lane plan is [phase-3.md](phase-3.md), and the evidence is the [R3 research session](../_local/sessions/2026-10-21-r3-sound-to-patch/summary.md) (reports 01 prior art and models, 02 data and training, 03 inference and integration, 04 evaluation and the lane plan; the summary wins where it corrects a report). The solver's pipeline is [design.md §8](design.md#8-solver-pipeline-sound--patch); the determinism rules are [design.md §5](design.md#5-determinism).

**Status (2026-10-21): planned; nothing is built.** Every decision here is provisional until you steer; work proceeds on the defaults meanwhile. FE, OBJ, TP, ONX, DG and TR add their sections as they land, and INTa and INTb keep this status line ([phase-3.md](phase-3.md#rules-for-every-lane)).

## Why R3 starts with search, and with the intake

- **Nobody turns arbitrary real sounds into patches well** (report 01). All published work trains on the synth's own renders, and out-of-domain error roughly doubles (Hayes 2025: spectral error 6.1 in-domain against 11.0 on NSynth and 15.4 on FSD50K). Nobody has tested degraded uploads. The one comparison of model + search with search alone is small and old: a warm start matched a GA in 40 % of its time (Shier 2021, 7 parameters). Synplant 2 publishes no method.
- **Today's search copies the damage** (report 04 §2, *measured*: `enth match` on one target, 400 renders, scored against the **clean** target):

  | Input | Sound % vs clean | What the search did |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | Clean | 98.98 (pass) | cutoff 6.90 kHz |
  | MP3 64 kbps | 91.92 (pass) | cutoff 4.58 kHz, copying the codec's low-pass |
  | Noise, 20 dB SNR | **10.27** | cutoff 58 Hz, release 4.46 s |
  | The same, tail trimmed to 1.8 s | 55.98 | cutoff 8.21 kHz, release 720 ms (truth 300) |
  | Reverb, RT60 1.2 s | **24.87** | release 1.56 s |
  | Clipped after +12 dB | **64.35** | cutoff 51 Hz, drive 6.2 dB, sustain 84 % |

  CMA-ES turns knobs to reproduce noise, reverb and clipping, and in model + search it would undo what an augmentation-trained model learned. One target and one seed is an existence proof, not a rate; lane OBJ repeats it on 40 targets × 4 degradations. So **R3a needs a robust intake and a degradation-aware objective before it ships**, and GR3 judges degraded uploads against the clean original.

## R3a: create from sound with search alone

### The intake (lane FE)

Pure functions in `enth-analysis/src/intake/` (`trim`, `floor`, `bandwidth`, `clip`, `perf`, `resample`), wasm32-clean, which describe what the upload still carries:

- mono is the mean of the channels; leading silence (about 60 dB below the peak) is stripped with a few ms of pre-roll; the length is capped at 4–6 s (evaluation cost grows with it);
- the noise floor, the bandwidth (a 64 kbps MP3 low-passes near 11 kHz), and clipped spans are estimated and reported as `Degradation`s;
- the performance is inferred (`InferredPerf`): the note from YIN, the note-off from the existing `infer_note_off`, the hold; several onsets raise a "this looks like a phrase" warning; `--perf` overrides it;
- **short sounds are never rejected**, and nothing is ever filtered by duration.

Acceptance: trim within 5 ms; bandwidth within 1/3 octave; the note right on at least 95 % of pitched clips; a chord warning; short clips kept. There is **no resampler in the product path**: render and analysis run at the file's own rate (8–192 kHz), as `enth match` already does, so whether `resample.rs` is needed at all is FE's open check.

### The robust objective (lane OBJ)

A degradation-aware distance in `enth-analysis`, under **a new metric id** that OBJ names; `sound/1` and every score already pinned stay unchanged. Its intent: compare a candidate with the upload only where the intake says the upload still carries information (the trimmed span, the band below the detected cut-off, levels above the noise floor, unclipped frames), so the search stops paying knobs to reproduce damage. OBJ designs it and proves it: on 40 dev targets × 4 degradations, the median `sound/1` against the clean target improves on v1.1, and clean inputs stay within 1 point.

**The degradation policy** (yours to decide; the default): codec damage, hum, noise and device EQ count as damage to ignore; for room reverb and compression, which the patch can also make, the K candidates include a dry patch and one using the patch's own reverb or compressor, and you choose by ear.

### The start portfolio and k-NN retrieval (lane TP)

Every create job starts from several places at once:

- **the heuristic start**, always kept ([dsp/match.md](dsp/match.md#the-heuristic-heuristicrs): pitch → tuning, centroid and tilt → filter, envelope → ADSR, noisiness → noise);
- **the templates** (`content/templates/`: `init`, `pluck_1osc`, `pad_2osc`, `bass_sub`), because structure is never searched otherwise (GR1's level 5: 0 of 4);
- **k-NN retrieval** over a pre-rendered patch bank (report 01 §7: 50–200 k generator patches, matched on a PCA'd pooled log-mel, later on the model's own embedding). TP fills the `nearest.rs` stub with it, which keeps that stub's meaning ("the nearest preset as a start"). Retrieval needs no training, gives several candidates, and is the baseline every learned model must beat. **The bank's size, features and delivery are TP's open check;** the default is seeds plus f16 vectors in a versioned file beside the plugin, regenerated by an xtask command, and R3a runs without it.

### K branches and successive halving

- Each candidate is its own `Search` (the existing ask/tell CMA-ES, which already takes one start and has `cancel()`). Each round merges every live search's `ask()` batch into one pool batch and splits the distances back to each `tell()`, so **the outcome does not depend on the worker count**; 8 searches × λ 16 = 128 jobs a round keep a pool busy.
- **Successive halving** is deterministic: all K get about 10 % of the evaluations, the best half the next 30 %, the best two the rest. Budgets double, as IPOP already does, so one long run gives the whole anytime curve.
- **Scale:** Phase 0's toy synth gave about 600 evaluations/s on 8 workers (about 36,000 in 60 s); GR1's floors suggest about 200 renders/s on 4 workers for R1's engine (12,000 in 60 s). TP measures the per-evaluation cost with effects before K and the budget are fixed.
- **Time:** the solver reads no clock, so the wall-clock cap lives in the shell's `search_with` callback (as `--max-seconds` does): it stops at the cap minus the intake. The front end (decode, trim, features) aims at 0.2 s, inference (R3b) at 1 s, and the rest of a 60 s target goes to search; **the first candidate arrives within 15 s**, and a result never takes more than 5 minutes.

### What lane TP built (F1s, 2026-10-21)

The design above, as built in `enth-solver/src/create/`, `nearest.rs` and `tools/datagen/src/bank.rs`. Everything in the solver is pure (no threads, clocks or I/O; wasm32): the shell passes the clip, the templates, the bank's bytes and an index-ordered `Objective`.

**The schedule** (`create/schedule.rs`):

1. **Round 0 screens every start once** (at most the budget's worth), so K proposals have distances after one batch, well inside the 15 s to the first candidate. The heuristic start is always kept; the best K − 1 others become the branches, in start order.
2. **Each branch is its own `Search`** from its start over what the held phrase reveals (`observable`, as `enth match`'s default), with the target's heuristic and a seed derived from the job's seed and the start's index.
3. **Each round** concatenates every eligible branch's `ask()` in branch order and splits the distances back by index.
4. **Successive halving in renders:** the budget left after screening is split 10 / 30 / 60 % over three rungs; within a rung each live branch gets an equal share, ending at a batch boundary; after rung 0 the best half survive (at least the finalists), after rung 1 the two finalists. A rung's budget comes from what is left when it starts.
5. **The budget is hard:** a round that would pass it keeps whole batches, best branch first, and is the last.
6. **`set_budget`:** between the screening round and the first rung's first batch, the shell may size the budget from the rate it measured on round 0 (renders per second × seconds left). The record stores the budget the rungs ran under, so a replay under it is the same run. This is how a time cap and halving agree: a budget the cap later truncates would leave the later rungs unspent.

Tests (`crates/enth-solver/tests/create.rs`): K branches and identical results for 1 and 8 workers (real renders through a thread pool); a slow, uneven objective and callback change nothing, and a cap after round 5 is a prefix of the full run; the rung boundaries fall where the render counts put them and every budget from 1 to 3,000 is respected; the heuristic start survives a screening it loses; a record replays the run from its starts alone; `set_budget` then a replay under the recorded budget gives the same result; a 10 ms clip is kept. The JSON round trip, lenient read and replay of `enth.create/1` are in `tools/datagen/tests/create_record.rs` (the solver cannot add `serde_json`), and the enums' wire names are insta snapshots.

**k-NN retrieval** (`nearest.rs`, embedding `knn-logmel-rel/1`):

- **The embedding:** metric v1's log-mel (−23 LUFS, 1024-point resolution), pooled over four segments from the note-on (0–0.1, 0.1–0.3, 0.3–0.8, 0.8–2 s; a segment past the clip's end reads as the floor, so short sounds stay valid), read at 28 third-octave bands **relative to the measured pitch** (half the pitch up to 256 times it; a fixed C3 for unpitched clips, so noise keeps absolute bands). Bands below 30 Hz or above 16 kHz, half the rate or **the intake's detected bandwidth** are masked. One byte per band (1/16 neper ≈ 0.54 dB; code 0 is masked): 112 bytes.
- **Why pitch-relative:** the bank renders each patch once, at C3; a query at any note matches by harmonic shape, and the neighbour is transposed by its measured pitch offset (whole semitones on every oscillator) before the search's heuristic fixes the cents. A PCA was not needed at 112 bytes, and masking needs the bands.
- **The distance** is the mean absolute difference over the bands both carry, computed on the bytes in integers and compared by cross-multiplication: the ranking is the same on every platform for the same codes; ties go to the lower index. Neighbours closer than `min_spread` to one already taken are skipped, so the starts differ.
- **Does it help?** `cargo xtask bank probe`: 100 held-aside generator targets (the bank's stream far past any entry, levels 2–5, played at G3 for 1.5 s, not at the bank's C3), metric v1 of each start **before any search**:

  | Start | Median distance, 2,000-entry bank | Median distance, the 20,000-entry bank |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | the init template | 1.820 | 1.820 |
  | the best of the 4 templates | 0.921 | 0.921 |
  | 8 random bank entries, the best | 0.845 | 0.944 |
  | the nearest neighbour | 0.935 | 0.890 |
  | the best of the 8 nearest (spread 0.25) | 0.615 | 0.660 |
  | **the best of the 16 nearest** (spread 0.25) | **0.553** | **0.589** |
  | targets where the best of 16 beats the best template | 97 of 100 | 93 of 100 |

  Retrieval is worth its screening renders, but only as a set: the nearest neighbour alone is no better than a template, and the screening round picks among the neighbours, so `neighbours` defaults to 16 (20 screening renders with the templates, 0.5 % of the budget). A bank ten times larger does not do better on these targets: the embedding's resolution, not the bank's size, is the limit. These targets come from the bank's own generator; real sounds will be harder (EVK tunes on dev).

**What it sizes.** With effects, an evaluation costs about 50 ms per second of clip on one worker, and 8 workers give about 4.3×. A 60 s job on 8 workers is about **5,000 renders for a 2 s clip and 2,000 for a 5 s one** (the plugin's `min(cores/2, 8)` = 6 workers here: about three quarters of that), far below the 12,000–36,000 the plan assumed. Hence: the default budget is 4,000; K stays 8 (rung 0 then gives each branch about 50 renders, its probe and a generation or two, and pruning there ranks the probes; the finalists get about 1,200 each); and **the evaluation clip is the main lever**: FE's 5 s cap costs 2.5× a 2 s clip, so whether the objective scores the whole capped clip or its first 2–3 s is a decision for OBJ and SK3 (open below). The shell should size each job's budget from its own screening round (`set_budget`) rather than from this table.

### The cancellable pool (lane SK3)

The plugin's `enth-game` worker is joined on drop ("a few hundred ms at most"), so a 60 s job there would hang an unload. Create jobs get their own:

1. **an `enth-create` orchestrator thread**, spawned at the first job and joined on drop, like `enth-game`: it decodes, computes features, runs inference on one thread (milliseconds) and drives the searches. It never runs on the audio thread and never holds the session lock while computing; results are posted the way the game worker posts them;
2. **a per-job `rayon::ThreadPool`** (never the global one), named `enth-search-N` and dropped when the job ends, so no threads exist between jobs; workers `min(available_parallelism / 2, 8)` at normal priority, never real-time;
3. **cancel:** an `AtomicBool` checked per job in the pool and after each round, so a cancel lands within one batch (about one evaluation, ~10 ms);
4. **gated under load:** `enth-test-host process-stats --gate g1` with a create job running, as lane SKR gated the A/B, because isolated benchmarks mislead under DAW load.

### File input

- **Decoders** (L8 only): WAV (`hound`, already in the workspace), FLAC (`claxon`), Ogg Vorbis (`lewton`), MP3 (`nanomp3`, a pure-Rust port of the CC0 minimp3), and a small in-house AIFF reader. `symphonia` (adds M4A/AAC and ALAC) is MPL-2.0, which `deny.toml` does not allow: **only with your OK**; the default is no M4A.
- **Drop a file on the editor:** baseview 0.4.1 delivers file drops on X11, Windows and macOS, but egui-baseview drops them on the floor. Fork patch 11 (lane FK3) maps `DragEntered`, `DragMoved` and `DragDropped` to egui's `hovered_files` and `dropped_files` and returns `AcceptDrop(Copy)`.
- **The fallback** is a folder browser inside the editor (no `rfd`, which would need a portal or GTK backend on Linux). Whether REAPER drags media items into a plugin window as files is an open check.

### The Create view and the session

- **`enth-session/src/create.rs`** is pure, like `game`: `Run` and `Cancel` go out to the shell, results come back. **"Use this" is one undo step** (`ops_between`, delivered like a preset load, with no host writes). A create job is not saved in the project (provisional; the F1s judge may ask otherwise): the patch you take is.
- **The view** (`enth-ui/src/editor/create/`, behind a feature like `recreate`): a drop zone showing the file's name, length, detected pitch and the intake's warnings; **K candidate tiles**, each with its distance and state (proposed → searching → done or pruned); one progress bar (renders against the budget, time left) and Cancel; per tile ▶ audition and A/B against the upload (Recreate's `Mailbox` and `Player`, level-matched) and "Use this". Branches keep both the proposal and the refined patch, Synplant-style, so you hear what the start gave and what search added. It repaints only on a worker post while visible.

### `enth create`

`enth create --target x.wav --candidates 8 --seconds 60 [--perf …] [--model m.bin]`, with JSON `"schema": "enth.create/1"` when stdout is not a TTY: the intake report, every candidate (patch, distance, provenance: heuristic, template, k-NN or model; its trace of renders and time), the K starts as patches, the input features' hash and the decoded audio's hash. CLC adds the flags, insta snapshots and [agent-cli.md](agent-cli.md)'s section.

## R3b: the first model

### `ds1`: training data from our engine (lane DG)

Renders are bit-exact, so a dataset is a manifest (a sampler version, seeds, a phrase sampler, an augmentation config) and clean audio never needs shipping or backing up. **Generator v1 is unfit as it stands** (level 1 has about 12 sounds, every phrase starts on C3 with holds of at least 1.5 s, `amounts` below 100 pull values towards the start), so a new versioned sampler, `ds1`, reuses v1's keyed draws, priors and `observable()` mask and adds:

- **a mixture** (a starting point to tune on dev): 60 % v1 priors at `amounts` 100 and level 5, structure included, with `musical` drawn from {100, 50, 0} at 0.6 / 0.25 / 0.15; 25 % perturbed preset candidates (σ 0.05–0.2 in the normalised domain plus a few categorical flips), **from the anchor half only**; 15 % uniform over the safe ranges;
- **a phrase sampler:** key log-weighted over MIDI 24–84, velocity 30–127, hold log-uniform from 0.08 to 4 s with envelope caps tied to it, 10 % octave or legato pairs, a fixed 3–4 s window from the onset;
- **labels:** values in the `ParamSpec` normalised domain (the ML boundary), one-hot categoricals, symmetric slots canonicalised, a loss mask from `observable()` (gauges, nuisances and dead parameters get no loss), the note nearest f0 with the smallest |semi| plus ±50 ct of note noise;
- **filters:** renders below −60 LUFS rejected; clipped renders kept; never a filter by duration;
- **splits by stream key** (`r3/train`, `r3/dev`, `r3/test`), never index ranges, so the sets are disjoint by construction; dedup by an exact hash of the masked canonical labels, then by pooled log-mel embeddings within about one just-noticeable step, re-run against every dev and test item after each refresh.

**The preset candidates are split into two disjoint halves:** anchors for `ds1`, and test targets for GR3, so no prior is fitted on a test preset. **Cost:** the CPU is the bottleneck (10–28 ms per dry 3 s render, 130–175 ms with the reverb, 65–110 ms per codec round trip); plan on 1–2 M renders consumed per run, a cache of about 250 k clean clips (48–72 GB) plus about 100 k pre-baked codec variants (about 19 GB), with 5–10 % of the shards re-rendered per epoch. Where `ds1` lives (default `tools/datagen`) and whether features reach `ml/` through `enth-py` or files are DG's open checks.

### Augmentation and licences (lanes DEG, AUG)

The chain follows a real signal path; 10–20 % of samples stay clean (report 02 §4):

| # | Stage | p |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Layer or mix-in: a second render at −6 to −20 dB, or a music bed at −10 to −25 dB | 0.15 |
| 2 | Trim and pad: cut into the attack, truncate the tail with a fade, add leading silence | — |
| 3 | Room: a real or synthetic impulse response, wet −20 to 0 dB | 0.3 |
| 4 | Device EQ: high-pass 80–400 Hz, low-pass 3–12 kHz, 1–3 peaks of ±9 dB, tilt ±3 dB/oct | 0.4 |
| 5 | Noise at 15–50 dB SNR; hum at 50/60 Hz and harmonics | 0.3 / 0.1 |
| 6 | Dynamics: **our compressor**, a look-ahead limiter, hard or tanh clipping at +3 to +12 dB | 0.2 each |
| 7 | Stereo to mono: gain imbalance, partial polarity cancellation | 0.1 |
| 8 | Band limit: resample to 8–24 kHz and back | 0.3 |
| 9 | Codec: MP3 32–128 kbps, AAC-LC 32–96 kbps, Opus 12–64 kbps, **pre-baked offline** in batches | 0.4 |
| 10 | Bit depth: 8–12 bit, 8-bit µ-law | 0.1 |
| 11 | Normalise to −23 LUFS (the in-house BS.1770) | 1 |

- **Held out for evaluation only:** a different MP3 encoder (shine), phone codecs (AMR, GSM), and rooms and noises from other collections (the MIT IR Survey, DEMAND), so GR3 measures generalisation, not memorised damage.
- **Degraded synth inputs are scored against the clean render:** we want the instrument back.
- **Licences:** shipped weights train only on our renders plus CC0, CC BY or Apache data (NSynth, MUSAN, FSD50K's CC0 and CC BY clips, BUT ReverbDB, OpenSLR 28, TinySOL, Freesound CC0 and CC BY; CC BY sources credited in `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`). CC BY-NC, CC BY-SA and licence-less sets (ESC-50, DEMAND, the MIT IR Survey, FSD50K's NC and Sampling+ clips) stay evaluation-only and local. LAME, shine and ffmpeg (LGPL, or GPL as distro-built) run only in `ml/`, as subprocesses, never in a bundle or CI artefact; fdk-aac is skipped; pedalboard (GPL-3.0) and gpuRIR (AGPL-3.0) are never imported. Open: the licences of AMR, GSM, WHAM! and OpenSLR 28's sources, and Freesound's terms on training.

### Real audio is a hypothesis

The roadmap's "unlabelled real recordings train the audio side" is a hypothesis to test, not a plan: in 2026 papers real audio helped through render rewards (GRPO in DDSynth-RL, SynthRL) and barely as a passive input (about 617 k unpaired clips added almost nothing in Hayes 2026). The first use is **search-distilled pseudo-labels**: model + search on real one-shots, keep the good fits as labelled pairs, retrain, capped at about 30 % of the data and listened to batch by batch. A learned embedding proxy is close to the banned "differentiable copy of the engine", so it waits for your OK (default no).

### The model: propose v0 (lane TR)

| Part | Choice |
| --- | --- |
| Input | About 2 s from the onset at −23 LUFS: `enth-analysis` log-mel (64 bands, 30 Hz–16 kHz, v1.1 pooling, 2–3 resolutions) at the file's own rate, plus f0 and loudness tracks; pitch-relative (`semi` predicted against the detected note). An MFCC input is worth an ablation (Shier) |
| Encoder | A small 2D CNN, about 1–4 M parameters, run once; it also exports its embedding for retrieval |
| Heads | Softmax for each enum, toggle and enable flag; route presence and depth on a fixed source × destination grid (no slot symmetry); continuous values in the normalised domain |
| Candidates | **K = 8 hypothesis head sets, winner-takes-all** (annealed or relaxed) **plus a confidence head** that ranks them before rendering: K hypotheses in one forward pass |
| Loss | Masked by `observable()`; oscillators canonicalised by pitch then level, LFOs by rate (or the min over the two permutations); continuous error in JND units (25 ct; 10 % for times and Hz; 2 dB) with a Huber loss; cross-entropy for discrete heads |
| Pipeline | Render all K, rank, refine by successive halving with R3a's other starts; **the heuristic start is always a candidate**, and the cap includes inference |
| Must beat | Search alone at equal wall-clock (GR3), k-NN retrieval, and itself without search (a diagnostic) |

**Why not flows or diffusion first:** they are the best published for symmetric spaces, but take 20–100 network evaluations per sample with models of about 40 M parameters, and flow matching lost to autoregressive decoding on a discrete-heavy FM synth (DDSynth-RL). If WTA collapses or fails GR3 in-domain, move up: conditional flow matching or masked discrete diffusion, then render-reward fine-tuning, then partial noising of the input at inference.

### Our own scalar kernels (lane ONX)

Inference runs in `enth-solver::propose` (L5, pure: no I/O, threads or clocks; wasm32), with the weights passed in as bytes:

| File | What |
| --- | --- |
| `pack.rs` | `ModelPack::parse(&[u8])`: the versioned header, sha256, fixture tests |
| `ops.rs` | Conv1d and Conv2d (stride, padding, dilation, groups), Linear, LayerNorm (BatchNorm folded at export), ReLU, GELU, SiLU, tanh, sigmoid, max, average and global pooling, softmax, residual add and concat, multi-head attention as Linear + matmul + softmax: a fixed summation order, no `mul_add`, transcendentals through the pinned `libm` |
| `net.rs` | `Net::run`: the layers listed in the pack |
| `features.rs` | The model's input from `enth-analysis`'s log-mel on the scalar FFT path |
| `decode.rs` | Outputs → patches: normalised → plain through `ParamSpec`, onto the template's free set |

- **Budgets:** at most about 4 M parameters (f16 in the file, at most 8 MB; f16 → f32 is exact), at most about 2 GFLOP per inference, the encoder run once. int8 only later, with integer kernels written to be bit-exact.
- **Measured** (report 03, a 0.8 M-parameter CNN, loaded machine): `tract` 62 ms, `rten` 18–29 ms, our naive scalar code 159 ms, all far under the budget; a blocked version with a fixed order would likely be 2–3× faster and stay bit-exact (an estimate). Bit for bit over 512 outputs: our kernels 512/512 on x86-64, `target-cpu=native` and wasm32; `tract` native against wasm32 37–48/512; `rten` changes with its thread count.
- **ONNX** stays the export and parity format: ONNX Runtime in `ml/`, and a dev-only `tools/model-check` (exempt) that compares our kernels with ONNX Runtime and `tract-nnef` within 1e-5 on exported fixtures and pins our exact output hashes on every CI platform.
- **Fallback** if the model outgrows the op set: `tract-nnef` only (never `tract-onnx`, which does not build for wasm32), behind an `infer-tract` feature. It needs your OK for `cc`, adds about 15 MB per plugin binary and gives up bit-exactness. **Rejected** for the product: `ort` (a C++ library), `candle`, and `rten` in the core (its own thread pool).

### The model file

- A separate, versioned file, `enth-model-<id>.bin`, beside the plugin; embedded only if it stays at 2 MB or less (bundling copies the weights into both the CLAP and the VST3 binary). The header: format version, model id, template id and version, the ordered parameter addresses and their normalisation, the feature config, the engine version, sha256.
- The plugin looks next to its binary, then in `<Documents>/enth/Models/`; the CLI takes `--model`. A missing file, or a header that does not match this build's catalogue and template, means "Create from sound" runs R3a and says so; a newer format is refused like `code_too_new`.
- No network code: an update is a plain file download, like a content pack. The R2 pack format may carry models later.

### Training

PyTorch with uv in `ml/`, never shipped and in no cargo or CI build; features computed by `enth-analysis` (through `enth-py` or files `tools/datagen` writes), never re-implemented in Python. On the laptop's GPU overnight with FP16 autocast, under a memory cap, never beside heavy builds, and attended the first time; a rented GPU only if a run would exceed about 24 h, and only on your account (a first model plus ablations is about 20–40 GPU-hours). Every run has a manifest (commit, engine version, `ds1` version and stream keys, phrase and augmentation configs, per-sample seeds `derive(run_seed, id, epoch)`, `ffmpeg -version`, cache and `uv.lock` hashes) and a 1,000-clip augmented golden that catches tooling drift. Logs stay local. The artefact is the model file plus a 20-input output golden.

## The determinism contract

[design.md §5](design.md#5-determinism) has the rule; here it is in full.

1. **Inference is bit-exact on every platform.** Our kernels use plain `+` and `×` in a fixed order, no `mul_add`, and the pinned `libm`; each model file has exact output hashes, checked on Linux, Windows, macOS arm64 and wasm32.
2. **Search is deterministic given its starts**, its settings and its render budget, for any worker count: merged batches are split back by index, and successive halving's schedule is a function of evaluation counts, never of time.
3. **The wall-clock cap only truncates.** A result records the renders it used, so a render-budget replay (the "render-equivalent twin" GR3 uses) reproduces it on any machine.
4. **Every result records its K starts as patches**, the input features' hash and the decoded audio's hash (`enth.create/1`, and the plugin's candidate list), so a run replays from its starts even where a lossy decoder differs in the last bits across CPUs (WAV and FLAC decode exactly; whether `nanomp3` and `lewton` do is an open check).
5. **Snapping proposals** to a grid (report 04's R8) is an optional guard; with bit-exact kernels it is not needed, and with a drifting runtime it would not suffice (about three quarters of runs would still cross a rounding boundary).
6. **Training is not bit-reproducible** (GPU kernels); the artefact that must be reproducible is the hashed model file and its output golden.
7. **No resampler in the product path:** render and analysis run at the file's own rate, and the model's features put their bands in Hz, so a low-rate file just has empty top bands.

## GR3 and its hygiene

**The gate** ([roadmap.md](roadmap.md#r3--sound--patch-610-weeks)): on held-out sounds from our own synth, model + search beats search alone at equal time; on real samples, including held-out degraded uploads, a blind listening test prefers it; every result arrives in under 5 minutes. Report 04 §3 makes each clause testable; EVK and EVb run it, and `docs/gr3.md` (planned) will hold the protocol's details and the runs.

**Clause 1: held-out synth sounds, equal time.**

- **Arms** share the intake, the start set, the free set, the workers and the seeds: **A**, R3a's best configuration tuned on dev (the baseline must be the best search, not GR1's 1,500-render floor); **B**, model + search from the same budget; **C**, the model's proposals unrefined (a diagnostic); **D**, A given the true template (separates structure error from parameter error).
- **Equal time:** B's clock includes intake, features and inference. Timed runs hold `flock -x` with no lane running, at the plugin's worker count (4) and the CLI's (8), and log renders for a render-equivalent twin.
- **Endpoints, fixed before the first test run:** at 60 s, the pass rate (`sound/1` ≥ 90 against the target patch) by McNemar on discordant targets, and the per-target difference in JND units (Wilcoxon, Hodges–Lehmann interval). Secondary: anytime curves (median at 10, 30, 60 and 300 s), time to pass (Kaplan–Meier, censored at 300 s), `structure/1`, results per level and per degradation. **"Beats"** means better at 60 s (one-sided p < 0.05) and not worse at 300 s.
- **Goodhart guard:** both arms minimise a log-mel distance and `sound/1` is log-mel, so one metric no arm optimises is reported too (MFCC, or a CLAP cosine on final candidates).
- **Size and cost:** a sign test needs 69 targets to detect a 65 % win rate (37 for 70 %, 158 for 60 %), so about 120. 120 targets × 2 arms × 60 s is about 4 h of the machine alone, plus about 7 h for a 40-target 300 s tail: overnight, two or three full runs at most.

**Clause 2: blind listening on real and degraded sounds.**

- **60 references** in `content/gr3/` (the manifest is committed; audio only when it is CC0 or yours): 10 one-shots from other subtractive synths, 10 FM, wavetable or digital, 10 acoustic and percussive (short sounds under 200 ms included, never filtered by duration), 10 of your picks, and **20 degraded copies, judged against the clean original**.
- **The held-out chain** (seeded, its parameters in the manifest, impulse responses, noises and codec settings disjoint from training's): MP3 at 32–96 kbps, noise at 5–20 dB SNR plus hum, reverb RT60 0.4–2.5 s, clipping and limiting at +6 to +18 dB, phone-speaker EQ, stereo to mono; plus about 30 phone re-recordings of yours if you make them (the default is synthetic phone EQ).
- **The trial** (`enth-listen gr3`): the reference, then A, then B; "which is closer to the reference?", forced choice. Each arm's **top-ranked** candidate by its own objective, never a human pick. Every clip, the reference included, at −23 LUFS (the in-house BS.1770), a trial with a clip over −1 dBTP lowered as a whole, the A/B order counterbalanced. Six catch trials (two identical pairs, four against the Init patch); a listener under 80 % on attention is excluded. Friends get `--export-kit`: random-named, loudness-matched WAVs and an answers file for any OS and player.
- **The decision rule, fixed in advance:** B is preferred when the 95 % cluster-bootstrap lower bound (resampling references, then listeners) is above 50 % and the degraded subset's point estimate is at least 50 %. Strata are reported, not gated.
- **Size:** you hear 60 references plus 6 catch trials (20–25 min, resumable); two friends hear 30 each (about 15 min). Owner-only detects about a 70 % preference. A MUSHRA-style rating on 10 references with all four arms is optional and on the cut list.

**Clause 3: under 5 minutes.** A cap guarantees it, so the gate is on what the cap hides: the maximum time from file to last candidate over every test run, with the test host playing a 16-voice patch; **time to the first candidate at most 15 s**; and `process-stats` p99 and p99.9 with a search running (`--gate g1`), so a search never costs the DAW an xrun.

**The sets** (lane DS, frozen and hashed in wave 1, before any training):

| Set | Size | Source | Leak guard | Used by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Generator targets | 60 (12 per level); DS may draw more to keep about 120 targets | Generator v1, a seed range training never draws | Dedupe against training within 1 JND | Clause 1 |
| Hand-made | About 30 renders at 2 notes: the test half of the 22 preset candidates, and your four G1 sounds once extracted | `content/presets-candidates/`, `content/presets/` | Never fit priors on them; disjoint halves | Clause 1 |
| Degraded synth | The clean targets through 4 held-out degradations | The held-out chain | Scored against the clean target | Clause 1, per degradation |
| References | 60 (40 clean in four strata, 20 degraded) | Synth one-shots, NSynth's test split, TinySOL, Freesound CC0/CC BY, your picks | Freesound-id dedupe against FSD50K; per-clip licence | Clause 2 |
| Dev | 30 | Disjoint streams and files | All tuning happens here | — |
| `r3/test` stream | About 2,000 | `ds1`'s own stream key | Never trained on; the dedupe radius | Development curves (report 02's set A) |

**Hygiene:** the sets, their hashes and the exclusion lists freeze in wave 1, and DG asserts that no training item is on them. Every test run is appended to `content/gr3/runs.jsonl` (build, date, load), and none is dropped. Tuning happens on dev only. The listening key stays closed until every listener is done, and whoever prepares stimuli does not audition them.

## Risks

Report 04 §4 has fourteen, each with its cheapest retirement. The ones that shape the plan:

| Risk | Retirement |
| --- | --- |
| Search fits the degradation and undoes the model | FE and OBJ before R3a ships; arm C; clean references |
| The domain gap: synth-trained proposals miss real sounds | R3a measures the real baseline first; a 10-sound pilot listen after the first small model; per-stratum reports |
| `sound/1` has no JND scale for a recording (it needs the target *patch*) | Raw distance and components for real audio; listening decides; an independent metric |
| Symmetric solutions make a regressor average modes | K winner-takes-all hypotheses; canonical slots; `structure/1` and best-of-K |
| Leakage into the held-out sets | The wave-1 freeze; disjoint preset halves; Freesound-id dedupe; disjoint impulse-response and noise pools |
| Real time and lifetime in the DAW | The separate cancellable pool; capped workers; `process-stats` under search |
| A wrong inferred performance (note, hold, chords) | The intake infers and warns; `--perf` overrides |
| Training compute on a laptop with tight RAM | Small models; overnight runs never beside builds; attended first; renting only with your OK |
